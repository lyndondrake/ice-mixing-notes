Ice-mixing notes

This site publishes the results of ice-mixing, a research project on spin ice on the pyrochlore lattice: how fast the standard local dynamics (single hexagon flips) relaxes, which ice states are frozen (admit no hexagon flip at all), and whether every frozen state of zero flux is periodic. The results are issued as stand-alone contribution notes, each with a PDF. For every note that has a claims manifest, its principal results are listed below one by one: what is asserted, on which cells (the periodic boxes of the lattice on which it holds), with what standing (proved, certified by a machine-checked proof, computed, conjectured, open), what it rests on, how to regenerate its evidence, and who wrote it and who checked it. Most of the work was done by language-model agents (Anthropic's Claude models) directed by Lyndon Drake; every claim names the model that wrote it and the independent checker that read it, and says whether a person has read it line by line.

32 claims in 1 manifest; 10 notes in all (9 without a published manifest yet). Claims read line by line by a person: 0 of 32.

Open problems

Claims whose status is open or conjecture: the targets the notes name.

  • Theorem H open theorem-h-orientation/thm-h

    In a frozen state with a hexagon of odd curl, some axis is empty (no vertex has that type) or one-way (every vertex of that type has the same sign of moment).

  • Theorem U open theorem-h-orientation/thm-u

    In an odd frozen state every octahedral hole is oriented: one of its rods points towards it and the other away. Equivalent forms: an odd state has no charged hole; two consecutive vertices of type \(k\) on a \(k\)-line have the same sign; a state with an unoriented cage has even curl; and the working form (V), that in a state with the four vertices of Theorem U′ the sublattice of the bridgeheads has no vertex of the rods’ type.

The status vocabulary

Every claim has exactly one of these. They are the project's own, defined in the manifest schema and discussed in the note on the method.

proved
a written argument read by an independent checker with its own programs.
proved-unchecked
a written argument with no second reader.
certified
a machine-checked proof object (DRAT, LRAT, a CPOG-certified count, Lean, Isabelle) on the cells named in scope.
solver
a SAT or LP verdict on named cells without a checked proof object.
computed
an exact computation with no solver verdict.
explored
floating-point or sampled evidence only.
conjecture
believed, with evidence named.
open
a target with no verdict.
closed-route
a method shown not to reach the target.
corrected
a statement of an earlier version repaired here.
refuted
shown false, with a witness.
