B1: the kernel of law \(P^k\)

The law at an A-hole

Let \(O = a + 2e_x\) with \(a \in\) A. The bridgeheads with \(s_x = -1\) are \(a + (1, s_y, s_z)\) with \(s_y s_z = +1\), that is \(a + d_0\) and \(a + d_1\). Those with \(s_x = +1\) are \(a + (3, s_y, s_z)\) with \(s_y s_z = -1\), that is \(a + 4e_x + d_2\) and \(a + 4e_x + d_3\). So law \(P^x\) at \(O\) reads \[K(a + 4e_x) - K(a) = K(a{+}4e_x{+}d_2) + K(a{+}4e_x{+}d_3) - K(a{+}d_0) - K(a{+}d_1). \tag{1}\] The inversion \(v \mapsto (1,1,1) - v\) exchanges A and B, maps holes to holes, reverses every \(s\) (and hence the product condition), maps each chain family to itself, and maps law \(P^x\) at a B-hole to law \(P^x\) at an A-hole. A permutation of the coordinates permutes \(\{d_0,\dots,d_3\}\), the families and the laws. So statements checked at A-holes for \(k = x\) hold at all holes and for all \(k\).

Lemma M (the modes solve the law; proved)

The indicator of a chain of axis \(y\) or \(z\) satisfies (1), and so do \(\mathbf 1_A\) and \(\mathbf 1_B\).

Proof. A B vertex \(b\) lies on a chain \(C\) of family \(\{i,j\}\) iff \(b - d_i \in C\) iff \(b - d_j \in C\). Take \(C\) of family \(\{0,2\}\). Then \(a + d_0 \in C\) iff \(a \in C\), and \(a + 4e_x + d_2 \in C\) iff \(a + 4e_x \in C\). Also \(a + d_1 \in C\) iff \(a + d_1 - d_0 = a - (0,2,2) \in C\), and \(a + 4e_x + d_3 \in C\) iff \(a + 4e_x + d_3 - d_2 = a + (4,-2,2) \in C\). These two points differ by \((4,0,4) = 2u_{02}\), so the last two terms of (1) cancel and (1) holds. The other three families are the same computation. For \(\{1,3\}\) the pairs are \((a+d_1, a)\), \((a+4e_x+d_3, a+4e_x)\), and \(a + (0,2,2)\) against \(a + (2,2,0)\), which differ by \(u_{13}\). For \(\{0,3\}\) they are \((a+d_0, a)\), \((a+4e_x+d_3, a+4e_x)\), and \(a - (0,2,2)\) against \(a + (2,0,-2)\), which differ by \(u_{03}\). For \(\{1,2\}\) they are \((a+d_1, a)\), \((a+4e_x+d_2, a+4e_x)\), and \(a + (0,2,2)\) against \(a + (2,0,2)\), which differ by \(u_{12}\). For \(\mathbf 1_A\), both sides of (1) vanish at an A-hole. At a B-hole the methylene difference is 0 and the four bridgeheads have \(\sum_s s_x = 0\). \(\square\)

(Also checked by the exact product law × modes = 0 on 20 cells in h441, and symbolically in h442 (3).)

Fourier reduction (proved)

Let \(G = \Lambda / (4a\mathbb Z \times 4b\mathbb Z \times 4c\mathbb Z)\), of order \(4abc\). For each character \(\psi\) of \(G\) fix an extension \(\chi(p) = x^{p_1}y^{p_2}z^{p_3}\) with \(x^{4a} = y^{4b} = z^{4c} = 1\). The functions \(e_{\chi,A} = \chi\cdot\mathbf 1_A\) and \(e_{\chi,B} = \chi\cdot\mathbf 1_B\), one extension per \(\psi\), form a basis of \(\mathbb C^V\). Every system below has integer coefficients and is invariant under \(\Lambda\), and it has one orbit of rows per hole sublattice (and per bond direction), so it maps the plane \(\langle e_{\chi,A}, e_{\chi,B}\rangle\) into the corresponding character space on rows, through a small matrix \(M(\chi)\). Hence \(\ker = \bigoplus_\psi \ker M(\chi)\). The span \(W\) of the modes is \(\Lambda\)-invariant, so \(W = \bigoplus_\psi W_\psi\), and \(W_\psi\) is spanned by the \(\psi\)-projections of the generators. The projection of \(\mathbf 1_C\), for \(C\) of family \(f = \{i,j\}\) based at \(p_0\), vanishes unless \(\chi(u_f) = 1\), and is then \(\bar\chi(p_0)\,|C\cap A|/|A| \cdot (1, 1/\lambda_i)\) with \(\lambda_i := \chi(d_i)\). The constants \(\mathbf 1_A\) and \(\mathbf 1_B\) project to \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) at \(\psi = 1\) only. Integer coefficients make the real statement follow from the complex one. So it suffices to show \(\ker M(\chi) = W_\psi\) for every \(\psi\).

Lemma P (the partition; proved). Since \(u_f = d_i - d_j\), \(\chi(u_f) = \lambda_i/\lambda_j\), so \(f\) is supported at \(\psi\) iff \(\lambda_i = \lambda_j\). The set \(Z(\psi)\) of supported families is the set of pairs inside the blocks of the partition of \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) by the value of \(\lambda\). There are five cases. All distinct: \(Z = \emptyset\). One pair: one family. Two pairs: the complementary pairs \(\{01,23\}\), \(\{02,13\}\), \(\{03,12\}\) are exactly the two families of \(x\), \(y\), \(z\). A triple \(\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{\ell\}\): three families, one of each axis, those of the kagome plane \(\perp d_\ell\). All four equal: then \(\chi\) is trivial on \(\Lambda\) (generated by the \(d_i - d_j\)), so \(\psi = 1\). (This replaces the Hermite normal form classification of the report of 17 September by one line.)

The symbol of \(P^x\) (proved). Rows are the A-hole and B-hole laws, columns \((\mathrm{amp}_A, \mathrm{amp}_B)\): \[M^x = \begin{pmatrix} D & -U \\ -V & D\end{pmatrix},\quad D = x^2 - x^{-2},\quad U = \sum_{\prod s = -1} s_x\chi(s) = xp - q/x,\quad V = \sum_{\prod s = +1} s_x\chi(s) = xq - p/x,\] with \(p = y/z + z/y\) and \(q = yz + 1/(yz)\). Put \(X_m = m^2 + m^{-2}\). Then \(pq = X_y + X_z\), \(p^2 + q^2 = 4 + X_yX_z\), \(UV = pqX_x - p^2 - q^2\) and \(D^2 = X_x^2 - 4\), so \[\det M^x = D^2 - UV = (X_x - X_y)(X_x - X_z).\] Also \(X_x - X_z = x^{-2}(x^2z^2 - 1)(x^2z^{-2} - 1) = x^{-2}(\chi(u_{02}) - 1)(\chi(u_{13}) - 1)\), and in the same way \(X_x - X_y = x^{-2}(\chi(u_{03}) - 1)(\chi(u_{12}) - 1)\). So \(\det M^x \neq 0\) iff no family of axis \(y\) or \(z\) is supported. This is item 3. Eliminating \(\mathrm{amp}_B\) gives \((D^2 - UV)K_A = 0\) as an operator identity, which is the “wave equation” form. It is a product of four difference operators \(T_{u_f} - 1\) (times a shift), applied to the restriction to A. It is not a factorisation of \(P^x\) itself, and the kernel of the product is the sum of the kernels of the factors only because all these operators are diagonal on \(G\).

When \(M^x = 0\) (proved). \(D = 0\) iff \(x^2 = \varsigma \in \{\pm1\}\). Then \(xyz\,U = \varsigma y^2 + \varsigma z^2 - y^2z^2 - 1 = -(y^2-\varsigma)(z^2-\varsigma)\) and \(xyz\,V = \varsigma(y^2z^2+1) - y^2 - z^2 = \varsigma(y^2-\varsigma)(z^2-\varsigma)\). So \(M^x = 0\) iff \(x^2 = y^2 = \varsigma\) or \(x^2 = z^2 = \varsigma\). The first says \(\chi(u_{03}) = x^2y^2 = 1\) and \(\chi(u_{12}) = x^2y^{-2} = 1\), both families of \(z\). Conversely both families of \(z\) give \(y^4 = 1\) and \(x^2 = y^2 = \pm1\). So \(M^x = 0\) iff both families of \(y\) or both families of \(z\) are supported.

Proof of Theorem K. Fix \(\psi\). By Lemma M every supported mode vector lies in \(\ker M^x(\chi)\), and it is nonzero (first entry 1). (i) No family of \(y\) or \(z\) is supported: \(\det \neq 0\), so \(\ker = 0 = W_\psi\) (the constants occur only at \(\psi = 1\), where every family is supported). (ii) Some are supported but not both of one axis: \(M^x \neq 0\) and \(\det = 0\), so \(\dim\ker = 1\), spanned by any supported mode. (iii) Both families of \(y\) are supported (or of \(z\)), say \(f = \{0,2\}\), \(f' = \{1,3\}\): \(M^x = 0\) and \(\ker = \mathbb C^2\). The modes are \((1, 1/\lambda_0)\) and \((1, 1/\lambda_1)\). They are independent unless \(\lambda_0 = \lambda_1\), which together with \(\lambda_0 = \lambda_2\), \(\lambda_1 = \lambda_3\) makes all four equal, so \(\psi = 1\). There the constants give \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). In every case \(\ker M^x(\chi) = W_\psi\). \(\square\)

Proof of the dimension formula. \(\dim\ker = \#\{\psi: \det = 0\} + \#\{\psi: M^x = 0\}\). Write \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta\) for the conditions \(\chi(u_{02}), \chi(u_{13}), \chi(u_{03}), \chi(u_{12}) = 1\). The number of \(\psi\) satisfying a set of conditions is \(|G|\) divided by the order of the subgroup of \(G\) generated by the corresponding steps. - One condition: \(\langle(2,0,\pm2)\rangle\) has order \(2\,\mathrm{lcm}(a,c)\), giving \(2b\,g_{ac}\) characters, which is the number of chains of the family. Likewise \(2c\,g_{ab}\) for the \(z\) families. - \(\alpha\beta\): the subgroup \(\{(2p,0,2q): p \equiv q \bmod 2\}\) has order \(2ac\), giving \(2b\) characters. \(\gamma\delta\) gives \(2c\). - A cross pair, for example \(\alpha\gamma\): the two steps span \(P_1 = \Lambda\cap d_1^\perp\). Now \(v \mapsto v\cdot d_1/4\) maps \(\Lambda\) onto \(\mathbb Z\) with kernel \(P_1\) and sends the cell periods to \(a, -b, -c\), so \(\Lambda/(P_1 + \text{periods}) \cong \mathbb Z/g_3\) and there are \(g_3\) characters. The four cross pairs are the four kagome planes. - Any three conditions include both families of one axis and one of another, which forces the fourth (for example \(\delta = \alpha\beta/\gamma\)). With all four, \(\psi = 1\), a single character.

By inclusion and exclusion, \(\#\{\det = 0\} = (4b\,g_{ac} + 4c\,g_{ab}) - (2b + 2c + 4g_3) + 4 - 1\) and \(\#\{M = 0\} = 2b + 2c - 1\). The sum is \(4b\,g_{ac} + 4c\,g_{ab} - 4g_3 + 2\). The generators number \(4b\,g_{ac} + 4c\,g_{ab} + 2\), so there are \(4g_3\) relations: one at each non-trivial \(\psi\) of the four kagome planes (\(4(g_3-1)\)) and four at \(\psi = 1\) (six vectors in \(\mathbb C^2\)). \(\square\)

B2: the joint system

The signed modes (proved). For a chain \(C\) of family \(f=\{i,j\}\) put \(n = \tfrac12 u_f\) on its vertices and 0 elsewhere. Distinct \(d\)’s have \(d_m\cdot d_{m'} = -1\), so \(d_m\cdot u_f = 0\) for \(m \notin f\), while \(d_i\cdot u_f = 4\) and \(d_j\cdot u_f = -4\). The bond rule on a bond of direction \(d_m\) with one end or both in \(C\): if \(m \notin f\) both sides are 0; if \(m \in f\) both ends are in \(C\) and both sides equal \(\tfrac12 d_m\cdot u_f\). The component of \(u_f\) along the chain’s own axis is 0, and each other component is a multiple of \(\mathbf 1_C\), which solves the law of that axis by Lemma M. So the mode solves the joint system. In coordinates, \(\tfrac12u_{01} = (0,1,1)\), \(\tfrac12u_{23} = (0,1,-1)\), \(\tfrac12u_{02} = (1,0,1)\), \(\tfrac12u_{13} = (1,0,-1)\), \(\tfrac12u_{03} = (1,1,0)\) and \(\tfrac12u_{12} = (1,-1,0)\). (I first derived these as the vectors orthogonal to the two \(d_m\) off the chain, and only then noticed that they are the chain steps.)

Proof of Theorem J. Symbol per \(\psi\): unknowns \((n_A, n_B) \in \mathbb C^3\times\mathbb C^3\). Law rows: \((n_{A,k}, n_{B,k}) \in \ker M^k(\chi)\) for each \(k\). Bond rows: \(d_r\cdot n_A = \lambda_r\, d_r\cdot n_B\), \(r = 0,\dots,3\). The mode of \(f\) projects to \((u_f, u_f/\lambda_i)\). Cases by Lemma P: - All distinct: every \(\det M^k \neq 0\), so the kernel is 0. - One pair \(f = \{i,j\}\) of axis \(m\): \(n_m = 0\) (no family of the other axes). For \(k \neq m\), \(\ker M^k\) is the line of \((1, 1/\lambda_i)\), so \(n_B = n_A/\lambda_i\). The bond rows become \(d_r\cdot n_A(1 - \lambda_r/\lambda_i) = 0\). For \(r \notin f\), \(\lambda_r \neq \lambda_i\), so \(n_A \perp d_r\) for both \(r \notin f\) and \(n_A \in \mathbb C u_f\). The kernel is 1-dimensional and spanned by the mode. - Two pairs, both families of axis \(m\) with values \(\alpha \neq \beta\): \(n_m = 0\) and, for \(k \neq m\), \(M^k = 0\), so the \(m\)-free components are unconstrained by the laws. Taking \(m = x\): \(d_0, d_1\) project to \(\pm(0,1,1)\) and \(d_2, d_3\) to \(\pm(0,1,-1)\) in the \(yz\) plane, and the bond rows reduce to \((n_A - \alpha n_B)\cdot(0,1,1) = 0\) and \((n_A - \beta n_B)\cdot(0,1,-1) = 0\). That is two independent equations on four unknowns, so the kernel is 2-dimensional. The two modes lie in it and are independent. - A triple \(\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{\ell\}\) with common value \(\mu \neq \lambda_\ell\): for each \(k\) the two supported families of the other axes lie on different axes, so \(M^k \neq 0\) and \(\ker M^k\) is the line of \((1, 1/\mu)\). Hence \(n_B = n_A/\mu\) with \(n_A \in \mathbb C^3\). The rows \(r \neq \ell\) hold automatically and row \(\ell\) gives \(n_A \perp d_\ell\). The kernel is 2-dimensional and spanned by the three modes \(u_f \perp d_\ell\) (any two of which are independent). - All equal (\(\psi = 1\), \(\lambda_r = \lambda\)): all \(M^k = 0\) and the bond rows give \(n_A = \lambda n_B\). The kernel is 3-dimensional and spanned by the six \(u_f\).

So the kernel equals the span of the signed modes at every \(\psi\). No sublattice constant is needed: at \(\psi = 1\) the antisymmetric constant is killed by the bond rule. Dimension: the chains number \(4(a\,g_{bc} + b\,g_{ac} + c\,g_{ab})\), with one relation at each non-trivial kagome \(\psi\) (\(4(g_3-1)\) in all) and three at \(\psi = 1\), which gives \(4(a\,g_{bc} + b\,g_{ac} + c\,g_{ab}) - 4g_3 + 1\). \(\square\)

In components, with \(G_f(v)\) the weight of the chain of family \(f\) through \(v\): \[n_x = G_{02} + G_{13} + G_{03} + G_{12},\quad n_y = G_{01} + G_{23} + G_{03} - G_{12},\quad n_z = G_{01} - G_{23} + G_{02} - G_{13}.\] Compare the types: \(K_y = G^T_{01} + G^T_{23} - G^T_{03} - G^T_{12} + \sigma\alpha\) and so on, where both families of an axis enter the two transverse types with signs \((+,-)\). For the signs the two families of an axis differ: \((+,+)\) for \(\{0,1\}\), \(\{0,2\}\), \(\{0,3\}\) and \((+,-)\) for \(\{2,3\}\), \(\{1,3\}\), \(\{1,2\}\), in the component order \((y,z)\), \((x,z)\), \((x,y)\). So the answer to “do they share weights in the same way” is: in another way. Each chain has one signed weight, and it enters both transverse components.

(B2b) proof. Per \(\psi\) the bond rule alone forces \(n_A\) once \(n_B\) satisfies \(\sum_r\lambda_r(d_r\cdot n_B) = 0\). The rows \(d_r\) satisfy only \(\sum d_r = 0\), so \(D n_A\) must have coordinate sum 0. The kernel is therefore 2-dimensional unless \(\sum_r\lambda_r d_r = 0\), which (the \(d_r\) having that one relation) means all \(\lambda_r\) are equal, \(\psi = 1\), where it is 3-dimensional. So \(\dim\ker(\text{bond}) = N + 1\) on every cell. The joint kernel is smaller exactly at the \(\psi\) with all \(\lambda\) distinct (0 against 2) or exactly one pair (1 against 2). At \(\psi = 1\) the laws alone allow 6 and the joint system 3, so the laws never imply the bond rule. The bond rule implies the laws iff the cell has no \(\psi\) of the first two kinds. On \((1,1,1)\) the four \(\psi\) are the trivial one and three of type “two pairs” (h443), so it does. If some extent, say \(c\), is at least 2, then \(\chi = (i, 1, \zeta)\) with \(\zeta = e^{2\pi i/4c}\) is admissible, and \(\lambda = (i\zeta, i/\zeta, -i/\zeta, -i\zeta)\) are distinct because \(\zeta^2 \neq \pm1\). So every other cell has a generic \(\psi\). \(\square\)

Theorem D, the six directions (proved, given (C) and Lemma 3)

Let a frozen state have every hole oriented. By (C) (report of 17 September, confirmed by check4), \(K = \sum_C f(C)\,t_C + \kappa + \sigma_v\gamma\) with type modes \(t_C = e_y - e_z\), \(e_z - e_x\), \(e_x - e_y\) for chains of axis \(x\), \(y\), \(z\), \(\kappa = (1,0,0)\) and \(\gamma = (-\alpha-\beta, \alpha, \beta)\). By Lemma 3, \(n\) solves the joint system, so by Theorem J \(n = \tfrac12\sum_C g(C)\,u_C\). For a chain \(C\) of axis \(m\), the transverse directions split into two unordered pairs \(\pi = \{\delta, \delta'\}\) with \(\delta - \delta' = \pm\tfrac12u_C\). For \(\{0,1\}\) these are \(\{+e_y, -e_z\}\) and \(\{+e_z, -e_y\}\); for \(\{2,3\}\) they are \(\{+e_y, +e_z\}\) and \(\{-e_y, -e_z\}\). Orient each pair so that \(\delta - \delta' = +\tfrac12u_C\) and give it the weight \(a_\pi(C)\). The pair mode (\(+a\) on \(D_\delta\), \(-a\) on \(D_{\delta'}\)) contributes \(a(e_{\tau(\delta)} - e_{\tau(\delta')})\) to \(K\) and \(a\,u_C/2\) to \(n\). With \(a_{\pi_1} = (f+g)/2\) and \(a_{\pi_2} = (g-f)/2\) (for family \(\{0,1\}\), \(\pi_1 = \{+y,-z\}\) and \(\pi_2 = \{+z,-y\}\); the other families alike), the sum over the two pairs reproduces \(f(C)t_C\) and \(\tfrac12g(C)u_C\). Therefore \[D_\delta(v) = \sum_{C\ni v}\ \sum_{\pi\ni\delta,\ \pi\text{ a pair of }C} \pm a_\pi(C) + \tfrac12\kappa_{k} + \tfrac12\sigma_v\gamma_{k}\qquad(\delta = \pm e_k),\] with sign \(+\) when \(\delta\) is the leading member. Each weight is shared by exactly two directions whose difference is parallel to the chain, in the way that a type weight is shared by the two transverse types. Explicitly, with \(h_\pm = (f\pm g)/2\) per chain: \(p_y = h_+^{01} + h_+^{23} - h_-^{03} - h_+^{12} + \tfrac12\sigma\alpha\) and \(m_y = h_-^{01} + h_-^{23} - h_+^{03} - h_-^{12} + \tfrac12\sigma\alpha\) (superscripts give the family of the chain through \(v\)), and cyclically. The weights are real, not unique (the relations of Theorems K and J), and nothing here says anything about integrality. The statement is linear. It does not encode \(|n_k| = K_k\).

Proposition O (proved). Let every rod line be complete (Lemma 1: every even-curl state). For a rod line \(\ell\) of axis \(k\) and a hole \(O\) on it, the charge formula gives \(c(O) = \varepsilon(O{+}2e_k) - \varepsilon(O{-}2e_k)\), the jump of \(n_k\) along \(\ell\) (T1). Let \(u_k\) be, on each rod line of axis \(k\), the antiderivative of these jumps normalised to mean zero on the line, and 0 elsewhere. Then \(n_k - u_k = \sum_\ell \bar n_\ell \mathbf 1_\ell\), where \(\bar n_\ell\) is the mean of \(n_k\) on the \(k\)-line \(\ell\). Any function on one sublattice that depends on one transverse coordinate lies in \(\ker P^k\): at holes of the same sublattice the methylene difference vanishes, and at holes of the other sublattice the two bridgeheads with a given transverse coordinate have opposite \(s_k\). So by Theorem K it lies in the span, and hence so does \(\sum_\ell F(\ell)\mathbf 1_\ell\) for every separable \(F\). On the torus a function on the grid of lines is separable iff all its mixed second differences vanish. By Theorem 5 of the note, the mixed second difference of \(\bar n\) around a \(k\)-line of holes is \(\mp\) its total charge divided by the line length. Hence \(n_k \equiv u_k + \sum_\ell\rho_\ell\mathbf 1_\ell\) modulo the span, for any \(\rho\) with those prescribed mixed differences. The data are the jumps on the rod lines of axis \(k\) and the line totals of the charges. \(\square\)

Checks (h445_outside.py). T1 (jump law) held in 186 of 186 (state, axis) cases. “The line means are in the span” failed in 106 of 186, and it fails exactly when some \(k\)-line of holes has nonzero total charge (T4, 0 exceptions). The simpler description, \(n_k \equiv u_k\) from the charges on rod lines alone, is therefore wrong in general, and the correction term \(\rho\) is needed.

B4: the signed line fractions

Proof of Theorem F. Average \(n_k = \sum_f (\tfrac12u_f)_k G_f\) along the \(k\)-line through \(v\). A chain of family \(\{0,1\}\) has constant \(x\) and constant \(y - z\) modulo \(4g_{bc}\) on each sublattice. Moving along a \(y\)-line or a \(z\)-line through \(v\) changes \(y - z\) by multiples of 4, so it meets every chain of the family in the pair of planes of \(v\) equally often. The average of \(G_{01}\) is therefore \(F_{01}\), the mean weight of the \(\{0,1\}\) chains whose A-plane is \(x\) (for \(v \in\) A) or \(x - 1\) (for \(v \in\) B, since \(d_0\) has \(x\)-component \(+1\)). For \(\{2,3\}\) it is \(x + 1\) on B (\(d_2\) has \(x\)-component \(-1\)). The other families work the same way. Substituting the components of \(\tfrac12u_f\) gives the formulas of item 13. \(\square\)

So the three signed fractions carry six family profiles, two per coordinate. On one sublattice the \(x\)-profiles of \(\sigma_y\) and \(\sigma_z\) are the sum and the difference of \(F_{01}\) and \(F_{23}\). They are not tied to each other, but they tie A to B: given the A fractions, the B fractions are determined up to additive constants. The sum at a vertex is \(2F_{01}(x) + 2U_{02}(y) + 2W_{03}(z)\) + const on A, shifted on B, and it is not constant. The direction fractions are \((h_k/\ell_k \pm \sigma_k)/2\), separable in the same two coordinates.

What failed, and why

  1. The simpler description of the charge defect. My first guess was that in an even-curl state \(n_k\) is, modulo the span, the mean-zero antiderivative of the charges along the rod lines of axis \(k\). It fails in 106 of 186 cases, exactly when a line of holes along \(k\) has nonzero total charge. Those totals include holes whose rods lie on other axes. The correct statement (Proposition O) adds the term \(\rho\) from Theorem 5.
  2. B5 controls. The first control (“the same directions present, with no absences required”) was UNSAT for every pattern with three axes, because it is itself the three-types query that Theorem S excludes. It was replaced by dropping the constraints on one required axis, and all controls were then SAT.
  3. Two script bugs, found and fixed before use: a substitution error in check (2) of h442, and an undefined variable in h448.
  4. Hand proof of Lemma M. I first tried to derive it from the type chain-mode lemma of the record, through the derivation of \(P^k\) from (T2). That route works, but it rests on a lemma I did not re-read, so I replaced it with the direct four-line check above.
  5. No direct real-space proof. The kernel theorem is not a factorisation of \(P^k\) itself. The factorisation exists only after eliminating one sublattice (item 3), and the case analysis at the degenerate \(\psi\) is still needed.

Relation to the main loop’s claims

Nothing contradicts them. Four refinements: (a) Only the antisymmetric sublattice constant is needed beyond the chains, because \(\mathbf 1_A + \mathbf 1_B\) is a chain sum. (b) “The six indicators have the same linear form as the types” is true in the sense of Theorem D, but the sharing is different. Each chain carries two weights, each coupling two directions whose difference is along the chain. The signed part has no sublattice constant, and the two families of an axis enter with different signs. (c) The signed line fractions do not add to a constant. There are six profiles, not three, and the fractions of B are determined by those of A up to constants, which has no counterpart for the types. (d) The membership of \(n_k\) in the span, sampled in 34 states by the main loop, holds in all 1,357 corpus states and all 65 oriented samples, and is now a consequence of Lemma 3 and Theorem K rather than an observation.
