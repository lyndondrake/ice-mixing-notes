T1. Reading, step by step

Ingredients

It closes after \(2\,\mathrm{lcm}(b,c)\) steps, and a plane of A holds \(2bc\) vertices, so a pair of planes holds \(\gcd(b,c)\) such chains. So \(\Phi(x_0) = w_{01}(x_0, x_0+1) + w_{23}(x_0-1, x_0)\). This does not affect anything.

Lemma T′

CONFIRMED.

  • \(q(m) = [\varepsilon(m+1,n) - \varepsilon(m,n)] - c(m+n)\) is an integer when \(c\) is.
  • \(\varepsilon(m,n+1) - \varepsilon(m+1,n) = p(n) - q(m)\) is correct: the \(\gamma\) terms cancel.
  • The integer zero-sum argument forces \(q \equiv 0\) once \(p \not\equiv 0\).

The failure of Lemma T (T2)

CONFIRMED by exact rationals (h430).

  • With \(\alpha = \beta = (2/3, 1/3, 0)\) and \(\gamma = (-1/3, 0, -2/3)\), \(\varepsilon = 1\) exactly at (0,0), (0,1) and (1,0), and 0 at the six other points. It is not a function of \(i\), of \(j\) or of \(i+j\). The jumps of \(\gamma\) are \(1/3\), \(-2/3\), \(1/3\).
  • The record’s hypotheses (route proposal, §3) are ‘\(\varepsilon: \mathbb{Z}^2/\Gamma \to \{0,1\}\) … with \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) real functions on the closed lines’. \(\mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3\) with \(\Gamma = 3\mathbb{Z} \times 3\mathbb{Z}\) is such a torus. So Lemma T is false as stated, and the author’s reading is right.
  • The faulty step is the one the author names: ‘\(a\) takes a value \(\geq 1\) and a value \(\leq -1\)’ holds only for integer \(a\).
  • By enumeration, the 0/1 functions in the tri-separable span that are not functions of one of \(i\), \(j\), \(i+j\) number 8 on \(\mathbb{Z}_2^2\), 36 on \(\mathbb{Z}_3^2\) and 96 on \(\mathbb{Z}_4^2\) (correction 3).

Lemma J

Task point b. CONFIRMED. Redone:

  1. Since \(c(k_0) - q(m) \in \mathbb{Z}\) for all \(m\) (take \(n = k_0 - m\)), every \(q(m) \equiv \theta\). Then every \(c(k) \equiv \theta\).
  2. Each has zero sum over its period (period \(2a\) for \(c\), \(g_z\) for \(q\)) and no integral value. So each has a value \(\geq \theta\) and a value \(\leq \theta - 1\).
  3. \(\max c \leq 1 + \min q \leq \theta\) and \(\min c \geq \max q - 1 \geq \theta - 1\). So both take only the values \(\theta\) and \(\theta - 1\).
  4. Then \(g_z \theta \in \mathbb{Z}\). On a plane \(y = y_0\) the same jumps \(c\) occur, because \(x\) runs over every value of the parity of \(S\) there too, with \(-c\) in place of \(c\). That gives \(g_y \theta \in \mathbb{Z}\).
  5. \(\gcd(g_y, g_z) = \gcd(a,b,c)\), which divides \(\gcd(b,c) = 1\).

Edge cases:

  • \(g_z = 1\): \(Q\) is constant, so \(q = 0 \not\equiv \theta\). The contradiction is immediate, which the argument covers.
  • Thin cells: \(a = 1\) gives \(\gamma\) period 2. \(b = 1\) or \(c = 1\) gives \(g = 1\). Nothing breaks.
  • Observation (not a gap): the last step needs only \(\gcd(a,b,c) = 1\). \(\gcd(b,c) = 1\) enters Lemma J only through (S1).

Theorem S

Absorption of constants (task point d): CONFIRMED.

  • On \(S\), put \(\Phi_S := \Phi + \sigma_S\alpha\) and absorb \(\sigma_S(\alpha+\beta)\) into \(F_y\).
  • This is legitimate because every later use of \(F_y\) and \(F_z\) is through their separable form on planes of \(S\), where adding a constant keeps the form.
  • The jumps of \(\Phi_S\) do not change.

Case 1: CONFIRMED.

  • Lemma T′ applies on each plane \(z = z_0\), with \(\alpha = -P\), \(\beta = -Q\) and \(\gamma\) non-constant. \(\gamma\) is non-constant because \(x\) runs over every value of the parity of \(S\) on that plane. So \(P\) and \(Q\) are constant on the plane.
  • The sheet lemma on the full product (x, z) forces \(\kappa\) constant. The same holds for \(\kappa'\).
  • The two-value argument gives \(\kappa' = \kappa + 1\), so \(X = 0\) on \(S\), and each \(x\)-plane is all \(y\) or all \(z\).

Case 2 (task point c): CONFIRMED. I redid the congruences.

  • The equations are:
    • \(2t - 2s \equiv -d_x \pmod{4a}\)
    • \(2\epsilon t \equiv -d_y \pmod{4b}\)
    • \(-2\epsilon' s \equiv -d_z \pmod{4c}\)
  • They fix \(t\) modulo \(2b\) and \(s\) modulo \(2c\). Then \(t - s\) runs over a coset of \(2\gcd(b,c)\mathbb{Z} = 2\mathbb{Z}\).
  • Its parity is that of \((d_y + d_z)/2\), and the target \(-d_x/2\) has the same parity because \(d_x + d_y + d_z \equiv 0 \pmod 4\). So a solution modulo \(2a\) exists.

Theorem S2

CONFIRMED.

  • \(F_y = \Upsilon(y)\) needs \(\gcd(a,c) = 1\) and \(F_z = \mathrm{Z}(z)\) needs \(\gcd(a,b) = 1\), both from (S1).
  • The sheet lemma on the full product (y, z) makes \(X\) a function of \(z\) alone or of \(y\) alone.
  • In \((P_z)\), \(Z = \mathrm{const} - F_x\) on \(S\). On each \(x\)-plane (a full product of the two families’ labels) it is constant along one family.
  • The line \(z = z_1\) of an \(x\)-plane has points 4 apart in \(y\) with \(y - z\) and \(y + z\) running over all classes modulo \(4\gcd(b,c)\), so it meets every chain of both families. Hence \(Z = 0\). \((P_y)\) and \((Q)\) follow the same way.
  • The notation ‘$Z = - F_x + $ const’ is consistent with $(y) = F_y + $ const, with \(\upsilon\) constant.

Corollary H

Task point e. CONFIRMED.

  • The rods of a hole \(O\) are its methylenes \(O \pm 2e_k\), which lie in the sublattice of \(O\). A rod on axis \(k\) has type \(k\). A corner has rods on two axes, so on \(S\) there are two types \(j \neq k\).
  • Theorem S makes \(S\) lack a type, necessarily the third type \(i\).
  • Theorem \(M'\) as stated in the note (line 455: ‘a frozen state in which one sublattice has no vertex of type \(k\) and the other has one has even curl’, extended to every cell in the subsection on non-cubic cells and by check6 §1) is exactly the form used.
  • ‘Odd ⇒ some corner’ is the forward half of Lemma 1. I checked that it holds when an extent is 1, where \(O + 2e_x = O - 2e_x\). \(N(O,\sigma) = 2\) still puts two rods of distinct axes in one octant.
  • Sampling agrees. Every odd state sampled on a cell with a coprime pair lacks a type. On (6,2,3), (6,3,4), (6,2,1) and (2,3,2) the absent type is the one Theorem S names. On (4,3,3) it is always \(x\), as the corollary of S2 says.
  • Scope correction (correction 1): Theorem \(M'\) is not certificate-free.

Corollary of Theorem S2 without Theorem M′

Task point f. CONFIRMED.

  • \(O' = O + \sigma\) is a B-hole when \(O\) is an A-hole, since \(\sigma\) has product \(+1\) and is one of the \(d_k\).
  • \(b_k = O' - 2\sigma_k e_k \in S'\). \(N(O', -\sigma) = 0\) says \(\tau(b_k) \neq k\).
  • \(b_x\) and \(b_y\) both have \(z = z_{O'}\), and \(b_x\) and \(b_z\) both have \(y = y_{O'}\). So \((P_z)\) and \((P_y)\) on \(S'\) each lead to a contradiction.
  • The step ‘a corner of \(S\) cannot have a rod of type \(x\)’ uses that the two rods of a corner share the coordinate of the axis neither of them lies on. That is correct.

Law of line fractions

CONFIRMED.

  • Averaging \(F_x\) along a \(y\)-line: the label \(y - z\) modulo \(4g_x\) of the {0,1}-chain runs uniformly over its \(g_x\) classes, since \(g_x \mid b\). The {2,3}-chain behaves likewise. The average is therefore the mean chain weight of the two pairs of planes through \(x\): a function \(P(x)\). Along a \(z\)-line the average is the same \(P(x)\), since \(g_x \mid c\).
  • \(F_z\) along a \(y\)-line gives \(R(z)\), and \(F_y\) along a \(z\)-line gives \(Q(y)\).
  • With the constants absorbed per sublattice (\(P_S = P + \sigma\alpha\), \(Q_S = Q + \sigma(\alpha+\beta)\)), the three formulas and their sum 1 follow.
  • ‘Even curl exactly when every fraction is 0 or 1’: forward by Lemma 1. Backward because a type-\(k\) vertex on an all-\(k\) line makes both rods of each adjacent hole lie on that line, so there is no corner and, by Lemma 1, no odd hexagon.
  • In every one of my samples the number of states with a fractional line equalled the number of odd states.

Encodings of the negation of Theorem S (‘A has a vertex of each type’), by translation and the half-turns

  • plain: the origin has type \(x\), and A has a \(y\) and a \(z\).
  • pair: A is FCC-connected, so two FCC-neighbours of A with different types exist. Translate one to the origin. The half-turns \(R_x\), \(R_y\), \(R_z\) (which preserve A, the bonds and the types) together with the reversal reduce \(\delta\) to \((2,2,0)\), \((2,0,2)\) or \((0,2,2)\) and the ordered pair of types to \(xy\), \(xz\) or \(yz\). The third type must then occur on A. That gives 9 queries whose disjunction is the negation.
  • Law C was added as a redundant cardinality constraint. It is a sum of (T2), so it is sound.
  • Controls:
    • plain: the same query without one of the ‘A has’ clauses. These must be SAT and were SAT on every cell.
    • pair: the same query without the third type. Four of these controls came out UNSAT: (4,3,3) pair 0 and 4, (6,2,9) pair 8, (7,3,6) pair 0 and 4. That is predicted:
      • an \(x\) next to a \(y\) along a \(z\)-plane diagonal is impossible under \((P_z)\) of S2;
      • a \(y\) next to a \(z\) inside an \(x\)-plane is impossible under Theorem S (i).
      They are consequences of the theorems and not encoding faults. The other pair controls were SAT.

Law of line fractions

Exact, with fractions.Fraction. The checks were:

  • \(f_y\) separable as \(P(x) - R(z)\);
  • \(f_z + P(x)\) a function of \(y\) alone;
  • \(f_x = 1 + R - Q\);
  • \(f_x + f_y + f_z = 1\) at every vertex.

  1. Summary item 2 (‘This needs no \(K_u\), no Theorem U, no Theorem R1 and no certificate’) is wrong in its last clause. Corollary H uses Theorem \(M'\). Theorem \(M'\) is proved (note, line 460; check6 §1) with finite lemmas on four hexagons that carry DRAT certificates: PLAQ_K, K4 and, for the first proof, PLAQ_C and EDGE_X. The certificates are window lemmas that pull back to every torus, so they do not limit the scope, but the corollary is not certificate-free. The route through Theorem S2 is free of \(M'\) and of certificates, but only on cells with \(\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(a,c) = 1\).
  2. Scope observation (not an error). In Case 2 of Theorem S the two lines meet whenever \(\gcd(a,b,c) = 1\): enumerated on (4,3,3), (5,3,3) and (3,4,4), where \(\gcd(b,c) \geq 3\). The last step of Lemma J likewise needs only \(\gcd(g_y, g_z) = \gcd(a,b,c) = 1\). The hypothesis \(\gcd(b,c) = 1\) is used essentially only through (S1), which is what makes \(F_x\) a function of the plane. The proof as written is correct. This locates what fails on cells without a coprime pair: (S1), not the meeting.
  3. The counterexample to Lemma T can be smaller. On \(\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2\) every 0/1 function is tri-separable (the span has rank 4). For example, \(\varepsilon = \delta_{(0,0)}\) with \(\alpha = \beta = (1/2, 0)\) and \(\gamma = (0, -1/2)\). Exhaustively, the failing 0/1 patterns number 8, 36 and 96 on \(\mathbb{Z}_2^2\), \(\mathbb{Z}_3^2\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_4^2\). The author’s \(\mathbb{Z}_3\) example is correct. The record should say that Lemma T fails on every square torus tried, and that its proof is right only for integer jumps.
  4. Minor (wording in the S2 proof). ‘$Z = - F_x + $ const’ is correct but relies on the reader identifying \(\upsilon\) with \(F_y\) up to a constant. It would read more safely as ‘\(Z = F_y - F_x + \sigma\beta\), and \(F_y\) is constant on \(S\)’.
  5. Thin cells (the author’s caveat 4) are not a problem for any step I read:
    • (T2) is taken on the lattice-derived hexagons of lattice.build, which raises on a degenerate hexagon and did not on (1,2,3), (2,1,3), (6,2,1) or (2,3,2).
    • Lemma 1’s forward direction survives \(O + 2e_x = O - 2e_x\).
    • Every structural claim held on the thin-cell samples.
