1. Part I, read by hand (T1)

The symbol of \(P^x\)

  • The law at an A-hole. At \(O = a + 2e_x\) the bridgeheads with \(s_x = -1\) need \(s_ys_z = +1\), which gives \(a + d_0\) and \(a + d_1\). Those with \(s_x = +1\) need \(s_ys_z = -1\), which gives \(a + 4e_x + d_2\) and \(a + 4e_x + d_3\). This is the note’s form of the law.
  • The A-hole row. Put \(K = \alpha\chi\) on A and \(\beta\chi\) on B, and divide by \(\chi(O)\). Since \(\chi(d_2) + \chi(d_3) = \xi^{-1}p\) and \(\chi(d_0) + \chi(d_1) = \xi q\), the row is \(\Delta\alpha - (\xi p - \xi^{-1}q)\beta\).
  • The B-hole row. At \(O = b + 2e_x\) the bridgeheads are \(b - d_3\) and \(b - d_2\) (with \(s_x = -1\)), and \(b + 4e_x - d_1\) and \(b + 4e_x - d_0\) (with \(s_x = +1\)). The row is \(-(\xi q - \xi^{-1}p)\alpha + \Delta\beta\). So \(M\) is as stated.
  • The determinant. \(\Delta^2 = X_\xi^2 - 4\) and \(\Theta\Theta' = X_\xi\,pq - (p^2+q^2)\). With \(pq = X_\eta + X_\zeta\) and \(p^2+q^2 = 4 + X_\eta X_\zeta\) this gives \(\det M = (X_\xi - X_\eta)(X_\xi - X_\zeta)\).
  • The factorisations. \(X_\xi - X_\zeta = \xi^{-2}(\xi^2\zeta^2 - 1)(\xi^2\zeta^{-2} - 1)\), which is \(\xi^{-2}(\chi(u_{02}) - 1)(\chi(u_{13}) - 1)\). Likewise \(X_\xi - X_\eta\) factors through \(u_{03}\) and \(u_{12}\). CONFIRMED.
  • When \(M = 0\). Take \(\xi^2 = \varsigma\), so that \(\xi^{-1} = \varsigma\xi\). Then \(\xi\eta\zeta\,\Theta = \varsigma\eta^2 + \varsigma\zeta^2 - \eta^2\zeta^2 - 1 = -(\eta^2-\varsigma)(\zeta^2-\varsigma)\), and \(\xi\eta\zeta\,\Theta' = \varsigma(\eta^2-\varsigma)(\zeta^2-\varsigma)\).
  • So \(M = 0\) if and only if \(\xi^2 = \eta^2 = \pm1\) (both families of \(z\)) or \(\xi^2 = \zeta^2 = \pm1\) (both families of \(y\)).
  • The converse also holds: \(\chi(u_{03}) = \chi(u_{12}) = 1\) forces \(\xi^4 = 1\) and \(\xi^2 = \eta^2\). CONFIRMED, and checked with sympy (h532).

Lemma 11

The complementary pairs are exactly the two families of one axis, and a triple \(\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{\ell\}\) contains one family of each axis. Equality of all four \(\lambda\) makes \(\chi\) trivial on \(\Lambda\), which is generated by the \(d_i - d_j\). CONFIRMED.

Lemma 12

At the A-hole, each family leaves two terms that must be the same chain (four families; each pair lies on one chain):

\(\{1,3\}\) \(a+(0,2,2)\) \(a+(2,2,0)\) \(u_{13}\)
\(\{0,3\}\) \(a+(0,-2,-2)\) \(a+(4,2,-2)\) \(2u_{03}\)

  • \(\mathbf 1_A\) and \(\mathbf 1_B\) satisfy the law.
  • The inversion maps \(\mathcal H_A\) to \(\mathcal H_B\) (using \((4,0,0) = u_{02} + u_{13} \in \Lambda\)), maps \(s\) to \(-s\), exchanges the methylenes, and maps law to law with the same sign. It fixes each family, since the bond \((a, a+d)\) goes to a bond of direction \(d\).
  • The permutations of the coordinates permute \(\{d_1, d_2, d_3\}\) and fix \(d_0\).

Theorem 16

  • The case analysis. For each character:
    • \(\det \ne 0\): the kernel is 0, and no family is supported.
    • \(\det = 0\) and \(M \ne 0\): the kernel is a line, spanned by any supported chain, whose vector is \((1, 1/\lambda_i) \neq 0\).
    • \(M = 0\): the two vectors of the two families of one axis are independent unless all \(\lambda\) are equal, and at the trivial character \(\mathbf 1_A\) and \(\mathbf 1_B\) span. The note writes this only for \(y\); \(z\) is identical.
  • The span decomposes. The span of the generators is \(\Lambda\)-invariant, so it is the sum of its projections, and those are spanned by the projections of the generators.
  • Real from complex. The real statement follows because the coefficients are integers and the generators are real: \(\ker_{\mathbb R}\otimes\mathbb C = \ker_{\mathbb C}\), and likewise for the span.
  • The count. I redid the inclusion–exclusion:
    • one condition: \(2b\gcd(a,c)\) and \(2c\gcd(a,b)\) characters;
    • \(\alpha\beta\): \(2b\), and \(\gamma\delta\): \(2c\);
    • cross pairs: \(\gcd(a,b,c)\) each, via \(v\mapsto v\cdot d_\ell/4\) onto \(\mathbb Z\) with the periods going to \(\pm a, \pm b, \pm c\). The four cross pairs are \(\perp d_1, d_3, d_2, d_0\).
    • Any three conditions give the fourth, for example \(u_{12} = u_{02} + u_{13} - u_{03}\). The four steps generate \(\Lambda\), since the graph on \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) with edges 02, 13, 03, 12 is connected.
    This gives \(\#\{\det=0\} = 4bg_{ac} + 4cg_{ab} - 2b - 2c - 4g_3 + 3\) and \(\#\{M=0\} = 2b + 2c - 1\), which sum to the formula.

CONFIRMED.

Theorem 17

  • The currents are solutions. \(d_r\cdot u_f = 0\) for \(r\notin f\). For \(r \in f\), a bond of direction \(d_r\) at a vertex of \(C\) is the chain’s own bond at that vertex, so both ends are on \(C\).
  • The five cases.
    • One family \(f\) of axis \(m\): law \(m\) has no supported family, so \(n_m = 0\). The other two laws give \(n_B = n_A/\lambda_i\). The two bonds \(r\notin f\) give \(n_A \parallel u_f\).
    • Two families (axis \(m\), values \(\mu \ne \nu\)): law \(m\) is non-degenerate and the other two symbols vanish. The bond rule reduces to \((n_{Ay} + n_{Az}) = \mu(n_{By} + n_{Bz})\) and \((n_{Ay} - n_{Az}) = \nu(n_{By} - n_{Bz})\) (for \(m = x\)). That is a plane, and \((u_{01}, u_{01}/\mu)\) and \((u_{23}, u_{23}/\nu)\) are independent vectors of it.
    • Three families: every law has two supported families on different axes, so \(M^k \ne 0\), \(\det = 0\) and \(n_B = n_A/\mu\). The bond rule on \(d_\ell\) gives the plane \(\perp d_\ell\).
    • Trivial character: \(n_A = \lambda n_B\), three dimensions.
  • The dimension. It is #chains − #relations, with relations \(0, 0, 1, 3\) in the four non-empty cases, which gives the formula. CONFIRMED.
  • Pressed points.
    • Does Theorem 17 need orientation? Theorem 17 is a statement about a linear system and has no hypothesis on holes. Orientation enters only in applying it to a state: by Lemma 3, \(n\) obeys all three laws at \(O\) if and only if \(c(O) = 0\), since one charged hole breaks all three laws by the same amount. So, for a frozen state, \(n\) is a sum of currents if and only if every hole is oriented. The note’s sentence ‘the moments of a state with every hole oriented are a sum of such currents’ is exactly right.
    • ‘No constant on a sublattice occurs.’ See correction 5. \(c\,\mathbf 1_A\) and \(c(\mathbf 1_A - \mathbf 1_B)\) are not solutions (checked for \(e_x\mathbf 1_A\) on all 16 cells). But the constant \(e_x\) on all vertices is \(\tfrac12\sum_{C\in\{0,2\}}\tfrac12u_{02}\mathbf 1_C + \tfrac12\sum_{C\in\{1,3\}}\tfrac12u_{13}\mathbf 1_C\).
    • Real from complex: as for Theorem 16.
    • ‘Neither system implies the other.’ Confirmed by hand: the bond rule alone has kernel dimension 2 at a character with non-equal \(\lambda\) and 3 at the trivial one. On 16 cells the kernel of the laws alone (exact, the sum of the three kernels of Theorem 16) always exceeds the joint kernel, so the laws never imply the bond rule. The kernel of the bond rule alone, computed modulo \(p\), equals \(N+1\) on every cell, as the hand count predicts; it equals the joint kernel on \((1,1,1)\) only, which is exact there and a mod-\(p\) statement elsewhere.

Theorem 18

Take the family \(\{0,1\}\), with (C) contributions \(f\) to \(Y\) and \(-f\) to \(Z\), and \(n\) contribution \(g(0,1,1)\). Then \(D_{\pm y} = \tfrac12(f\pm g)\) and \(D_{\pm z} = \tfrac12(-f\pm g)\).

  • The pair \(\{+e_y,-e_z\}\) has difference \((0,1,1) = \tfrac12u_{01}\) and weight \(\tfrac12(f+g)\).
  • The pair \(\{+e_z,-e_y\}\) has weight \(\tfrac12(g-f)\).
  • For \(\{2,3\}\) the pairs are \(\{+e_y,+e_z\}\) and \(\{-e_y,-e_z\}\), with weights \(\tfrac12(f+g)\) and \(\tfrac12(f-g)\).
  • The constants \(\tfrac12(\kappa_k + \sigma_v\gamma_k)\) come from (C) only.

CONFIRMED.

Theorem 19

  • The components. \(\tfrac12u_f\) gives \(n_x = G_{02}+G_{13}+G_{03}+G_{12}\), \(n_y = G_{01}+G_{23}+G_{03}-G_{12}\) and \(n_z = G_{01}-G_{23}+G_{02}-G_{13}\). All three are correct.
  • Where each family sits. On an \(x\)-line the chain of \(\{0,2\}\) through each vertex lies in the pair of \(y\)-planes of the vertex. Its chains in that pair are the classes of \(x - z\) modulo \(4\gcd(a,c)\), and the line moves \(x - z\) by 4 at each step through \(a\) vertices, a multiple of \(\gcd(a,c)\). So the average is the mean weight over the pair. This holds on every cell.
  • The shifts on B. A chain of \(\{i,j\}\) has its B vertices at its A vertices \(+\,d_i\). So at \(w\in\) B its A-plane along axis \(m\) is \(w_m - (d_i)_m\):
    • \(x\): \(d_0, d_1\) have \(+1\) and \(d_2, d_3\) have \(-1\), so \(F_{01}(x-1)\) and \(F_{23}(x+1)\);
    • \(y\): \(d_0, d_2\) have \(+1\) and \(d_1, d_3\) have \(-1\), so \(U_{02}(y-1)\) and \(U_{13}(y+1)\);
    • \(z\): \(d_0, d_3\) have \(+1\) and \(d_1, d_2\) have \(-1\), so \(W_{03}(z-1)\) and \(W_{12}(z+1)\).
    These are the stated shifts, in the stated order. CONFIRMED.
  • The sum. The sum on A is exactly \(2F_{01}+2U_{02}+2W_{03}\) (correction 6).

4. Corrections and gaps

  1. (dfour §2.10, ‘Which members’). ‘In L7 the planes \(z_0\pm2\) may coincide (extent 2 along \(z\), 8 planes)’ is false. On every member \(4c \ge 8\), and \(z_0 + 2 \not\equiv z_0 - 2\). The statement is not used.
  2. (dfour §2.4, the remark). ‘\(\sigma_k^S = 0\) on that plane (L1)’: L1 is not needed, since \(|\sigma_k| \le a_k\) always.
  3. (dfour L5b). The constancy of the \(y\)-part of \(\sigma^B_x\) (in (ii)\(_z\)), and of the \(z\)-part (in (ii)\(_y\)), is derived but not used. The contradiction follows from \(|\sigma^B_z| = \tfrac12\) (respectively \(|\sigma^B_y| = \tfrac12\)) alone. This is a simplification, not an error.
  4. (dfour §2.11). The flux identity is not needed. The curl note defines zero flux as equal numbers of \(+\) and \(-\) moments on each axis of each sublattice, which excludes a one-way axis with a vertex at once.
  5. (note, after Theorem 17). ‘No constant on a sublattice occurs’ should say that no constant on one sublattice alone (no \(\sigma_v\) term, unlike (C)) occurs. A constant vector field on all vertices is a solution and a sum of currents, for example \(e_x = \tfrac12\sum_{C\in\{0,2\}\cup\{1,3\}}\tfrac12u_C\mathbf 1_C\).
  6. (note, after Theorem 19). ‘Their sum on A is \(2F_{01}(x)+2U_{02}(y)+2W_{03}(z)\) up to a constant’: the identity is exact, with no constant.
  7. (note, Theorem 16 proof; wording). The case \(M=0\) is written for both families of \(y\). The case of both families of \(z\) (\(\lambda_0 = \lambda_3\), \(\lambda_1 = \lambda_2\)) is the same and should be named.
  8. (dfour L6, simplification). The inversion is not needed in L6: with \(F_\pm\) and \(U_\pm\) constant, (B) gives \(\sigma^B_z = \sigma^A_z\).
