Reading (conjecture). At \(g = 2\) the relaxation is closed by the x-circle exactly when the circle has an odd number of cubes. A relation of the form \(f(n) + f(n+1) = c_n\) round a circle determines \(f\) when the length is odd and leaves an alternating solution when it is even; the LP witnesses on even cells and on slabs are presumably of that alternating kind. The witness on (4,2,4) has x-mass exactly 6.4 on every A-grid and 4.8 on every B-grid (h351_lp.py 4,2,4 all --grids), and its \(y\) and \(z\) masses are not periodic with one cube, so the alternation, if it is one, is in the transverse structure and not in the grid counts. At \(g = 3, 4\) nothing of the kind happens. So the clean statement that \(g = 2\) needs integrality is: it needs it on every slab and on every cell of even rod-axis length; on odd rod-axis length it does not (computed on the cells listed). A slab certificate cannot use this, since a slab has no circle.

6. A hand proof at \(g = 2\)?

Not written. What the computations say about its shape:

  1. The integrality needed is one x-chain’s types (§2): a closed one-dimensional object of \(4\,\mathrm{lcm}(b,c)\) vertices, lying in two grids, the chain next to the rod’s chain. This is the analogue for (V) of uwall’s “one line of \(2L\) indicators” for the corner case, and it is uniform in \(g\) from 2 to 4.
  2. None of the three known non-linear steps fits as it stands. Lemma 4 needs two separable non-negative functions on a product; the chain is one-dimensional and the slab has no x-circle over which to integrate a separable count. The sheet step (\(F(i) + G(j) \in \{0,1\}\)) needs a function on a transverse product; the transverse torus of a chain slab is a single circle. Lemma T is about a single chain’s kinks at \(g = 1\); at \(g = 2\) the neighbouring chain is the second chain class of the slab, which is where a Lemma-T-type argument would have to act, but the split does not branch on the choice of chain class (§3).
  3. The parity phenomenon of §1 suggests a different kind of step: at \(g = 2\), a counting identity exists as soon as the x-direction closes up with odd length. A hand proof on the slab might therefore run: show by counting that on the slab some integer quantity attached to the neighbouring chain satisfies an alternating recurrence along the chain, and use its integrality (a parity argument) where the odd circle would have closed it. I have not identified that quantity. Conjecture: the step at \(g = 2\) is a parity step, of a kind the project has not yet used.

What failed, and why

  • Positivity of the bridgehead type (h354_pos.py): on (2,2), (2,4) the LP forces \(t(b,y) > 0\) at every vertex of the target grid, which with integrality would give (V) by one-sided splits. On (4,6) only 6 of 48 are forced positive, on (2,6) 20 of 24: a small-torus effect. The cumulative caterpillar on (4,6) needs all 48 vertices.
  • Lines and the cage’s methylenes as split sets: lines close only on thin tori, at positions that do not transfer ((0, ±2) on \(b = 2\), (0, ±6) on (4,6), not (0, ±10) on (6,8)); the eight indicators of the four transverse methylenes of \(O\) are not a uniform split on (2,6) or (4,6).
  • Reading the certificates: 49 of 65 branch certificates on (4,6) use most of the slab; I could not recognise a structure. The odd-\(a\) certificates use the whole x-circle.
  • Degree-two lift at \(g \ge 3\) and on (4,6): the full lifts are too large to decide in the time allowed; the thin lifts are feasible.
  • Greedy splits are scattered and torus-specific (as uhand found on cubic cells); only the restriction to the neighbouring chain gave sets with a common geometry, and those still lengthen with the torus.
