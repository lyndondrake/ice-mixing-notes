1. Reading, step by step (T1)

Everything below was recomputed by hand from the definitions. I did not rely on the report’s algebra.

§0 and Theorem P

  • Chains as residue classes. A-plane \(n\) has \(2bc\) A-vertices, and on it \(y-z\equiv y+z\equiv -n \pmod 4\). So \(y-z \bmod 8\) takes two values, each with \(bc\) vertices. This is well defined because \(4b\) and \(4c\) are multiples of 8. A chain of \(\{0,1\}\) has step \((0,2,2)\) and \(\mathrm{lcm}(2b,2c)=bc\) A-vertices (gcd 2). The two chains are therefore exactly the two classes. CONFIRMED.
  • (a) Chain signs. For each family I checked two things:
    • the exponent is an integer on A;
    • it is invariant under the chain’s step, and flips under the translation that exchanges the two classes.
    family exponent invariant under evaluates to
    {0,1} \((x+y-z)/4\) \((0,2,2)\) \(\pi\zeta\)
    {2,3} \((x+y+z)/4\) \((0,2,-2)\) \(\pi\)
    {0,2} \((y+z-x)/4\) — \(\pi\xi\)
    {1,3} — — \(\pi\)
    {0,3} \((z+x-y)/4\) — \(\pi\eta\)
    {1,2} — — \(\pi\)
    The signs are well defined on the torus because \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are even (translation by \(4a\), \(4b\) or \(4c\) moves the exponent by an even integer). CONFIRMED.
  • Signs at B vertices. For \(\{0,1\}\) through \(w-d_0\) the exponent differs from that of \(\pi_B\zeta_B\) by \(z-1\), which is even. For \(\{2,3\}\) through \(w-d_2\) it differs from that of \(\pi_B\) by 1. The same pattern holds for \(\{0,2\}/\{1,3\}\) via \(w-d_0\), \(w-d_1\), and for \(\{0,3\}/\{1,2\}\). The A-planes are \(n'\mp1\) as stated, so \(E^B_k(n',\sigma)=\sigma e(n'-1)-e'(n'+1)\). CONFIRMED.
  • Separability, P1, P2 and P3.
    • P1: \(\pi\) alternates along a \(k\)-line.
    • P2: every pair of values of two coordinates occurs at a vertex of \(S\). The profile algebra is correct, and the slacks sum to 2 identically.
    • P3: the parity rule, and its converse (\(a\)’s sum to 1 and \(b\)’s to 0).
    CONFIRMED.
  • Coupling. \(D^B(n')=2e_{01}(n'-1)=D^A(n'-1)\) and \(S^B(n')=-2e_{23}(n'+1)=-S^A(n'+1)\). CONFIRMED.
  • Freedom (i). \(m_{23}(n+2)-m_{23}(n)=\Phi^B(n+1)-\Phi^A(n)\) is solvable round the circle if and only if the sums agree. The constants \(c^{A,B}_{p,q,r}\), \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) then satisfy three sum conditions and four difference conditions, and these are consistent (one free constant remains). CONFIRMED.
  • Freedom (ii). Each variable \(E_k(n,\sigma)\) lies in exactly one class. Within a class every pair of planes occurs, which makes a complete tripartite graph, so there are four constants.
    • I checked the shift of A class \((+++)\) by \(c\) for the \(x\) and \(y\) components: B class \((+++)\) shifts by \(+c/2\) and the other three B classes by \(-c/2\), uniformly.
    • Computed, not just argued: the kernel of weights → \((Y,Z)\) has dimension 8 on five members. Its image on the E-data of A has rank exactly 4, and every kernel vector shifts every class of A and of B uniformly.
    CONFIRMED.

Step 1

\(X(v)=X(v')=1\) with opposite \(\pi\) gives \(a^A_x(0,0)=1\), so \(q_A(0)=r_A(0)\). The chain \(r(0)\le\max r\le\min p\le\max p\le\min q\le q(0)\) collapses. CONFIRMED.

Step 2

  • At \((x,y,0)\): \(\zeta=+\) and \(\eta=\xi\), so \(E_x(x,+)=E_z(0,\xi(x))\). This gives \(\kappa\) on planes \(x\equiv0\) and \(\lambda\) on \(x\equiv2\).
  • At \((x,0,z)\): \(\eta=+\), so \(\zeta=\xi\) and \(E_y(0,\xi)=E_x(x,\xi)\). This gives \(\mu\) on \(x\equiv2\), and also \(E_y(0,+)=\kappa\), which (A7) uses.
  • Class bookkeeping: \(\kappa\) is in \((+++)\), \(\lambda\) in \((-,-,+)\) and \(\mu\) in \((-,+,-)\). Every equation stays within one class.

CONFIRMED.

Step 3 (★)

  • Bridgehead classes. Recomputed: \(\beta\) is \((+++)\) with \(\pi=+\); \(\beta'\) is \((+,-,-)\) with \(\pi=-\); \(\gamma\) is \((-,+,-)\) with \(\pi=+\); \(\gamma'\) is \((-,-,+)\) with \(\pi=+\).
  • Collecting terms. The \(a\)-parts give \(p_B(1)-p_B(3)\). The E-parts give \(D^B_x(1)-S^B_x(3)+S^B_z(-1)-D^B_z(1)\), which by the coupling is \(D^A_x(0)+S^A_x(4)-2E^A_z(0,+)\). Substituting Step 2 gives (★).
  • Gauge. \(E_x(0,-)\) and \(E_x(4,-)\) are both in class \((+,-,-)\), so their difference is gauge invariant.
  • Consequence. The deduction of (V) from \(E_x(0,-)=E_x(4,-)\) (all other slacks 0) is correct.

CONFIRMED.

§2, other positions (claim 4)

The reflection \(x\mapsto4-x\) is not a lattice symmetry: \((1,1,1)\mapsto(3,1,1)\), whose coordinate sum is 5, is not a B vertex, and the bond vectors are sent to \(-d_i\). See correction C1. The half-turn \((x,y,z)\mapsto(4-x,-y,z)\) works in its place:

  • it fixes \(O\);
  • it preserves A, B, the bond set and every type;
  • it sends \(\beta\mapsto\gamma'\) and \(\beta'\mapsto\gamma\).

The other pieces are also right:

  • \(y\leftrightarrow z\) preserves A, B and the bonds, and maps the cell to \((a,c,b)\), which is again a member.
  • The inversion maps A-holes to B-holes.
  • Coordinate permutations move the rod axis, and the permuted cell is a member.
  • A-translations make all A-holes equivalent.

3. Corrections and gaps

  • C1 (correction, claim 4). ‘\(x\mapsto 4-x\)’ is not a symmetry of the diamond lattice: it sends B to a set that is not a sublattice, and the bond vectors to their negatives. Replace it by the half-turn \((x,y,z)\mapsto(4-x,-y,z)\), which fixes \(O\), preserves A, B, the bonds and the types, and exchanges the two sides of the cage. The step can also be dropped: \(y\leftrightarrow z\) already produces the other order of types, and translations and the inversion give every hole of either sublattice. The conclusion is unaffected, and the solver confirms all 24 positions per cell.
  • C2 (wording). ‘The torus only through the existence of the vertex classes on planes 1, 5, 3, −1 (so \(4a\ge8\))’ should also name the planes \(\equiv 2 \pmod 4\) along \(y\) and \(z\), and the distinctness of A-planes 0, 4 and 2, 6. All of these hold on every member.
  • C3 (observation, Step 5). The sub-case \(E_x(0,-)\neq E_x(4,-)\) of the special case is UNSAT even without the bridgeheads on (2,2,2), (2,2,6), (2,4,6) and (4,6,10). The proof reaches its contradiction through (★), which needs the bridgeheads, so the hand argument is valid but not the shortest.
    • The pinning and parity facts cannot be tested on solutions under this hypothesis. I checked them instead by the finite enumeration h475, which is non-vacuous and has 0 failures for both signs.
    • The special case itself is realisable (44 states), and (A3)–(A7) hold on all of them.
  • No gap was found in Theorem P, (★), Steps 1–5, or the derivations of Theorem U and of Theorem H on the thick members.
