## 3. Characterisation of `v_even`

### (a) Momentum

| translation | character on `v_even` | trace character on the slowest odd multiplet (deg 4) |
|---|---|---|
| `T_y` (one cell in y) | 1.000000000000 | −0.0 |
| `T_z` (one cell in z) | 1.000000000000 | +0.0 |
| `T_yT_z` | 1.000000000000 | −4.0 |

`v_even` is at **zero transverse momentum** — H4 TRUE.

The odd multiplet's traces are the genuine, basis-independent characters (the
individual `<u, T u>` are properties of an arbitrary Lanczos basis, not of the
chain). Trace 0 for each generator and −4 for their product forces the
four-dimensional multiplet to consist of two states with `(χ_y, χ_z) = (−1,+1)`
and two with `(+1,−1)`. Since `b = c = 2`, `q_min` along a transverse axis *is*
the zone boundary and `e^{iq_min·a} = −1`, so those are exactly the momenta of
`P_z(q_y)` and `P_x/y(q_z)`: **the slowest odd modes sit at the transverse
zone-boundary momenta, and the slowest even mode at zero momentum.**

### (b) Point group — derived, not assumed

`icemix.parity.lattice_automorphisms` derives the cell's affine automorphisms
from the coordinates: candidates `p ↦ M p + t (mod box)` with `M` a signed axis
permutation preserving the box lattice, accepted only if the map permutes the
site set, carries every bond to a bond, and carries the **derived hexagon set**
to itself. Where the map exchanges the A and B sublattices the arrow bit
complements, so the induced state map is `s ↦ π(s) ⊕ f` with an explicit flip
mask; that composition law is unit-tested against repeated application.

Two crystallographic anchors were asserted, not assumed:

- at `(1,1,1)` the construction returns **192** automorphisms — exactly the
  number of general positions of `Fd-3m` in one conventional cubic cell, with
  96 sublattice-swapping (a test);
- at `(1,2,2)` it returns **256** (16 point operations preserving the
  (4,8,8) box × 16 FCC-coset translations), **all 256 of which act on the giant
  component** — i.e. the giant component is a union of full symmetry orbits.

Every automorphism was verified to commute with `P` on the sector (a test at
`(1,1,2)`).

**The result.** χ(g) = `<v_even, g v_even>` is ±1 on every one of the 256
elements, and

    max_g | chi(g) - det(M_g) |  =  2.66e-14        (128 proper, 128 improper)

so `v_even` transforms in the **determinant (pseudoscalar) representation**:
invariant under all 128 proper operations, sign-reversing under all 128
improper ones. Deviation from the trivial character is 2.0 (i.e. it is not
totally symmetric); the stabiliser has exactly 128 elements. The second and
third even modes are neither pseudoscalar nor trivial (deviation 2.0 from both)
— they carry other one-dimensional characters. This is developed in §4.

### (c) Degree structure

Least-squares projection onto the span of spin monomials
`σ_S = ∏_{b∈S}(2x_b − 1)` under the uniform measure on the giant component
(the monomials are *not* orthogonal once restricted to an ice sector, so this
is a genuine Gram solve, done by symmetric eigendecomposition with an explicit
rank cutoff). `σ_S` has parity `(−1)^{|S|}`, so an even vector has exactly zero
overlap with every odd-degree monomial — measured **8.7e-20**, asserted.

| vector | degrees | columns | rank kept | fraction (rcond 1e-10) | 1e-8 | 1e-12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `v_even` | 0 + 2 | 2,017 | 402 | **0.853223** | 0.853223 | 0.853223 |
| 2nd even | 0 + 2 | 2,017 | 402 | 0.876672 | 0.876672 | 0.876672 |
| 3rd even | 0 + 2 | 2,017 | 402 | 0.820310 | 0.820310 | 0.820310 |
| `v_odd` (multiplet mean) | 1 | 64 | **30** | **0.597232** | 0.597232 | 0.597232 |

The fraction is identical across all three rank cutoffs, so the rank-deficiency
of the Gram is not doing the work — and it is severe: the 2,017 monomials span
only a 402-dimensional space of functions on the giant component, and the 64
single spins span only 30. **H1 is TRUE at 0.85322.**

Degree 3 (41,664 columns) and degree 4 (635,376 columns) were **not run**;
costs and reasons in §9.

### (e) Degeneracy and splitting

`v_even` is simple, split from the second even mode by 1.229e-3 in λ and from
the third by 1.275e-3. The even sector's first *degenerate* multiplet is 4-fold
at λ = 0.9942156099 (rank 5–8). The odd sector's slowest is 4-fold at
λ = 0.9958774927 and its second is 4-fold at 0.9954992346. So no near-degeneracy
threatens the identification of `v_even`.

### The pair amplitude

Since `v_even` is 85 % degree 2, its degree-2 content is described by the
symmetric 64 × 64 matrix `C_ij = <v_even, σ_i σ_j>`. Its diagonal vanishes
identically (`σ_i² = 1` and the eigenvector is orthogonal to the constant) —
measured 1.5e-18. `C` is **not** rank one: effective rank `‖C‖_F⁴/Σw⁴ = 18.35`,
top eigenvalue weight 0.0756, and the leading eigenvalues come in **± pairs**
(−0.002933, −0.002933, +0.002933, +0.002933), which is the signature of an
*antisymmetric* bilinear pairing rather than the square of one form. The six
leading eigenvectors each overlap 0.46–0.48 with a `P_j(q)` component and
0.24–0.47 with an odd eigenvector's spin profile, and each puts a quarter of its
weight on the bonds crossing one cut plane. That is exactly the structure of
`Im(P_i* P_j)` — §4.

## 4. Unexpected: the mode is the emergent magnetic helicity

This was not registered; it fell out of the character analysis and was then
tested directly.

For a divergence-free field the natural bilinear **pseudoscalar** is
`q · (P* × P)`: with `q` along axis `k` and `(i,j,k)` cyclic,

    h_k(q) = Im( P_i(q)* P_j(q) )
           = Re P_i · Im P_j  -  Im P_i · Re P_j .

It is even under `R` (a product of two odd factors) and changes sign under
every improper lattice operation (the cross product does) — the same
transformation law the character analysis found for `v_even`, and one that
nothing in the existing panel has. In the continuum this is the magnetic
helicity `∫ A·B` of the emergent gauge field.

Measured overlaps with `v_even`, and with the second even mode as a control:

| helicity observable | overlap `v_even` | overlap 2nd even mode |
|---|---|---|
| `h_y + h_z` at `q_min` (`helicity_yz`) | **0.75859** | 1.5e-25 |
| `h_y` at `q_min` | 0.43300 | 2.6e-27 |
| `h_z` at `q_min` | 0.43300 | 4.0e-25 |
| `h_x + h_y + h_z` at `q_min` | 0.42149 | 7.9e-26 |
| `h_y + h_z` at `2q_min` | 0.27991 | 5.5e-26 |
| `h_y − h_z` at `q_min` (staggered) | **2.3e-27** | 2.7e-25 |
| `h_x` at `q_min` (thin axis) | 0.03464 | 8.5e-27 |

The span of the eleven independent helicity columns captures **0.83456** of
`v_even` — against a total degree-2 content of 0.85322. In other words *the
helicity family accounts for 98 % of everything degree-2 in the mode.* It
captures **4.7e-22** of the second even mode (which is therefore a different
object entirely) and 0.81703 of the **third** even mode.

The symmetry bookkeeping closes on itself, which is the check that this is not
a coincidence. The `y ↔ z` swap `[[1,0,0],[0,0,1],[0,1,0]]` has `det = −1`, so
it must send `v_even → −v_even`; geometrically it exchanges `h_y ↔ h_z` *and*
flips the helicity's sign, so `h_y + h_z → −(h_y + h_z)` (odd, matches) while
`h_y − h_z → +(h_y − h_z)` (even, must be orthogonal). Measured overlap of
`h_y − h_z` with `v_even`: **2.3e-27**, i.e. exactly zero. Likewise `h_x`, the helicity
about the *thin* axis, is nearly decoupled (0.0346) — there is only one cell of
room along `x`, so there is no long-wavelength helicity to be had there.

### The exact autocorrelations, and the panel fix

`rho(t)` was computed exactly by 4,000 sparse mat-vecs for the panel and for
the helicity family. `tau_exp` below is the relaxation time of the slowest
eigenvalue the observable actually couples to; `tau_int` is the exact
integrated time.

| observable | parity | tau_int (att) | tau_int (sw) | tau_exp (att) | couples to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `P⊥(q_min)` (`Pz_qy`, `Py_qz`) | odd | 181.73 | 2.840 | **242.07** | slowest **odd** mode |
| `P⊥` (`Px_qy`, `Px_qz`) | odd | 158.92 | 2.483 | 221.68 | 2nd odd multiplet |
| `F(q_min)` (all four) | even | 49.78 | 0.778 | — | *nothing in the top 28* |
| `n_flippable_total` | even | 51.69 | 0.808 | 176.85 | a fast even mode |
| `abs2_P⊥(q_min)` | even | 105.77–124.64 | 1.653–1.948 | 263.13 | **2nd** even mode |
| **`helicity_yz`** | even | **309.02** | **4.828** | **389.45** | **the slowest mode of the chain** |
| `helicity_qy`, `helicity_qz` | even | 265.13 | 4.143 | 389.45 | the same |
| `helicity_total` | even | 193.49 | 3.023 | 389.45 | the same |

`1/Δ = 389.95` attempts and `tau_exp = -1/log λ = 389.45` attempts. So the
helicity observable's `tau_exp` **is** the true relaxation time, to the
last digit, while the whole existing panel tops out at 263.13 (the second even
mode) or 242.07 (the slowest odd mode) — short of the gap by a factor 1.61.
`F(q)`, the panel's designated even member, couples to *no* mode in the top 28
of either parity: its `rho(t)` decays on a fast local scale (`tau_int` = 0.78
sweeps), exactly the "invisible slow tail" the plan's §4(c) anticipated, but
for a symmetry reason rather than an amplitude one.

**What this says about the thin-cell pathology.** The three slow even modes at
`(1,2,2)` are not two-photon states of the Gaussian argument; they are
long-wavelength **helicity** (chirality) fluctuations of the emergent field,
which the plan's §4 argument does not contain and which no member of the
production panel can couple to. The ladder of §6 supports the reading that they
need two thick directions: the quasi-1D cells, which have only one, put the
even sector at Δ_even/Δ_odd ≈ 3.7–3.9 instead.

**What it does *not* say.** This is a statement about a 64-spin cell of
thickness 1. It does not establish that a helicity mode exists, or is slow, in
cubic geometry; §2 of the plan already records that exact diagonalisation
cannot reach cubic cells. What it does establish is that *the reason the panel
missed the slow mode at (1,2,2) is a symmetry the panel does not contain*, and
that adding one pseudoscalar member fixes it — see §8.6.

Two cautions travel with this table, both from stage 1/2: the `(1,1,c)` cells
are quasi-1D and stage 1 excluded them from anchoring anything, and at `(1,1,4)`
the giant component is *not* the component the production chain lives in
(stage-2 anomaly 2), so its numbers describe the giant component and not `D`.

Read structurally rather than as a trend in one parameter: **the anomaly is not
"thin cells" but "cells with a two-dimensional cross-section and only one cell
of thickness"**. The quasi-1D family sits at 3.7–3.9 and rises slowly with `c`;
`(1,2,2)`, the one cell in the reachable set with two thick directions, is the
only one where the even sector is slowest, and §4 says why: it is the only one
with a long-wavelength transverse helicity. (Inference, not measurement: a
ratio near 4 is what a *single* quantum at `2 q_min` would give under ω ∝ q²,
where two quanta at `±q_min` would give 2; this report does not test that.)

## 7. (1,2,3): what was measured, the projection, and the design

### Measured

`enumerate.frontier_count` with `track_axes=(0,1,2)` at `(1,2,3)`, in **14.9 s**,
peak 123,432 frontier keys:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| ice states | 3,076,306,362 |
| **`W = 0` states** | **196,465,480** |
| winding sectors | 453 |

Both counts match stage 1 exactly (asserted in a test).

### Projection — the reason it was not run

Scaled from the measured units of §1 (`mean flippable / n_hex = 0.1715` at
`(1,2,2)`, so 16.46 of 96 hexagons at `(1,2,3)`):

| item | projection |
|---|---|
| nnz of `P` | 3.23e9 |
| state array (2 × uint64) | 2.9 GiB |
| CSR indices (int32) | 12.0 GiB |
| CSR indptr (int64) | 1.5 GiB |
| Lanczos basis, `k=6`, `ncv=20`, one sector | 14.6 GiB |
| **total resident** | **31.8 GiB** — *inside* the 40 GB budget |
| Python DFS enumeration | 1.8–7.1 h (and the existing enumerator refuses > 64 spins) |
| adjacency build, 1.89e10 binary searches at the measured 1.91 µs | **10.0 h** |
| one mat-vec (10–40 ns/nnz out of cache) | 32–129 s |
| mat-vecs per parity sector (measured 973 at `(1,2,2)`; assume 1×–3×) | 973–2,919 |
| **eigensolve, both sectors** | **18.9–226 h** |

Total **31–243 h wall**. The budget is ~4 h. **Verdict: do not run** — as
instructed. Memory is not the binding constraint; the binary-search index and
the unaccelerated Lanczos iteration count are.

### Design for a later dedicated run

1. **Enumerator.** A C extension in the style of `icemix/_msc.c`, built through
   the same ctypes path: the same DFS with constraint propagation and the
   winding-slack pruning of `enumerate_ice_states`, with the state carried in
   two `uint64` words, the `Schedule` passed in from Python, and leaves written
   into a caller-preallocated `(N, 2) uint64` buffer (never a Python list —
   196 M Python ints alone is ~7.8 GiB). Expect ~100× the Python rate, so
   minutes. **Gates:** (a) the leaf count must equal `frontier_count`'s
   196,465,480 exactly; (b) at `(1,2,2)` the C enumerator's sorted output must
   equal `enumerate_ice_states(zero_winding_only=True)` bit-for-bit.
2. **Index.** Not `np.searchsorted` — 1.91 µs/lookup is 24 cache misses per
   lookup and costs 10 h on its own. Lexsort the `(N,2)` array once, then build
   an open-addressing hash table of ~2^29 slots (2.1 GiB, int32 payload) keyed
   on a mix of the two words. Target ~100 ns/lookup → ~31 min for the whole
   adjacency build.
3. **Adjacency.** CSR with *implicit* uniform weight (no `data` array): int32
   indices, int64 indptr, built hexagon by hexagon in a count pass then a fill
   pass. 13.5 GiB.
4. **Mat-vec.** A small C/OpenMP kernel for
   `y = (1 − n_flip/(2 n_hex)) x + (1/(2 n_hex)) A x` over that CSR. It is
   gather-bound on a 786 MB vector; **re-measure the DRAM-scale random-gather
   rate before committing** — the 10–40 ns/nnz bracket above is the largest
   uncertainty in the whole projection.
5. **Parity.** Do *not* materialise `P_even`/`P_odd` at this size. Run the
   solver on `P` with each iterate projected, `x ← (x ± x[R])/2`, which needs
   only the `R` index permutation (int32, 786 MB) and one extra gather per
   iteration.
6. **Eigensolver.** Plain Lanczos needs O(10³) mat-vecs because the top of the
   spectrum is clustered within 1e-3 of 1. Use LOBPCG with a small block and a
   Chebyshev polynomial accelerator on `P`, or a polynomial-filtered subspace
   iteration; shift-invert is out (no factorisation at this size).
7. **Operations.** Checkpoint the enumeration output, the CSR, and the solver
   basis to local NVMe — a 20 h run must survive. Submit through `expq` with
   `memory_limit` (32 GiB) and a canonical `ProgressReporter` feed carrying a
   per-mat-vec step counter, per the experiment conventions.

Note also that `(1,2,3)` still has thickness 1 in `x`, so — per plan §2 — it
would test the thin-cell pathology, not cubic geometry. Given §4 and §6, the
specific prediction a `(1,2,3)` run would test is *whether the helicity mode
stays slowest when one transverse direction is lengthened*, i.e. whether the
ratio moves back toward the quasi-1D value or stays below 1. That is a sharper
target than the ratio-toward-2 reading as registered.

## 8. Defects and deviations recorded

1. **`pyrun.sh` is unusable in this repository.** It refuses with "no `.venv/`
   found (searched from `~/repo/project/ice-mixing` to `/`)". The
   project has no virtualenv; stages 1–3 were run under the system
   interpreter (`~/.pyenv/shims/python3`, 3.12.13, numpy 1.26.4,
   scipy 1.17.1) and this run was too. Not a defect in the project, but the
   brief's instruction could not be followed as written and the deviation is
   recorded rather than hidden.
2. **`exptop` is not importable from the system interpreter.** It lives in its
   own venv at `~/repo/project/exptop/`. `grep -rl "class
   ProgressReporter" --include='*.py' . | grep -v .venv` over this repository
   returns **nothing** — there is no vendored copy here, and none was created.
   Stages 1–3 used a hand-rolled heartbeat writing `results/progress.json`,
   which is *outside* the exptop feed root and therefore invisible to exptop.
   Rather than vendor anything, the run appends
   `~/repo/project/exptop/src` to `sys.path` and uses the
   **canonical** `exptop.progress.ProgressReporter` alongside the repo-local
   heartbeat; `results.json` records which it got
   (`meta.canonical_progress_feed = "canonical"`). If exptop is absent the run
   degrades to the local heartbeat and says so.
3. **Per-writer temp filenames.** The repo's stage-1 `Progress` class has the
   heartbeat thread and the main thread writing the same
   `progress.json.tmp`, which races on `os.replace` and can kill the daemon
   thread silently. This run's copy uses per-writer temp names and wraps all
   progress I/O in `try/except`. The stage-1/2/3 scripts still carry the
   racy version; **not fixed here** (they are historical artefacts of completed
   stages) but recorded for the main loop.
4. **Basis-dependence trap, caught.** The first pass reported per-eigenvector
   overlaps for the slowest *odd* mode. That eigenvalue is 4-fold degenerate,
   so those numbers are properties of an arbitrary Lanczos basis, not of the
   chain — and indeed they moved between two runs (0.3887 → 0.3166) while the
   physics did not. Every odd reading was converted to a multiplet invariant
   (trace character; projection onto the invariant subspace) and the
   per-mode spread is now reported as evidence (1.2e-13).
5. **Two zombie exptop feed cards — my defect.** Runs 2 and 3 of the ledger in
   §1 were stopped with `tmux kill-session -t stage4d` while the script was
   being extended. `tmux kill-session` delivers **SIGHUP**, and the canonical
   `ProgressReporter` installs handlers for SIGTERM and SIGINT only, so its
   exit path never ran and the cards
   `ice-mixing-stage4d-1246242` and `ice-mixing-stage4d-1253319` were left
   reading `status: running` against dead PIDs — they show in exptop as hung
   jobs. Neither a SIGKILL nor a hang was involved; it was SIGHUP, which the
   reporter cannot catch as configured. The main loop stamped both `failed`.
   Corrective practice adopted for the rest of this task: stop a run with
   `kill -TERM <pid>` (or `tmux send-keys C-c`) and wait, never
   `kill-session`; stamp the card by hand after any deliberate kill; and open
   no feed at all for sub-minute probes (`preflight.py` opens none). No
   handler was added to `run_thin_even.py` — the reporter's own SIGTERM/SIGINT
   handlers are correct and duplicating them would be worse. **Upstream note
   for `exptop`:** a SIGHUP handler on the same exit path would have prevented
   this entirely, since `tmux kill-session` and terminal loss are the ordinary
   ways a detached run dies.

6. **Actionable for the project:** the production panel (`icemix/panel.py`)
   contains no pseudoscalar. At `(1,2,2)` every even member is *exactly*
   orthogonal to the slowest mode, so the panel's "slowest of the panel" is a
   lower bound that misses the gap by a factor 1.61 there for a reason no
   amount of statistics or series length fixes. Adding
   `h = Im(P_z(q_y)* P_x(q_y)) + Im(P_x(q_z)* P_y(q_z))` — three multiplies and
   two subtractions on numbers `measure_panel` already records — closes it:
   the exact `tau_exp` of that observable at `(1,2,2)` is **389.45 attempts**
   against a true `1/Δ` of **389.95**, where the best existing member reaches
   263.13. It costs no extra Markov time (it is a function of the polarisation
   components already buffered) and it is even, so it complements rather than
   replaces `P⊥`. Whether the cubic panel has the same blind spot is
   **untested** — this report cannot say, and §4 is explicit about that; the
   cheap way to find out is to add the member and compare, since a cubic
   `tau(h)` exceeding `tau(P⊥)` would be a finding on its own.
