## Verdicts

1. **(V) is not proved.** Nothing x-global is proved here. What *is* proved by hand is the
   cage's own content, and it is exactly law C: **Lemma U1** below writes out the four
   hexagons of the A-cage and shows that under yy/zz each of them already carries its two
   matches (one rod, one bridgehead), so all four transverse methylenes are unmatched. No
   further literal follows.
2. **The neighbouring cages give nothing (proved; computed).** Six B-holes of grid g+1 are
   adjacent to β or β′; exactly two of them are adjacent to both, and their cages carry three
   of the four constrained bridgeheads each. One of their four hexagons is a hexagon of the A-cage itself; the other three give
   the three equations (K2), (K3), (K4) of **Lemma U2**, in six new indicators, with four or
   five free terms apiece. None is a unit, and no literal is forced.
3. **No proof by local propagation (computed, `h120`).** Closing one-hot and "a hexagon has
   exactly two matches" to a fixpoint from the hypothesis determines 6 vertices and halves
   the type set at 4 more — precisely the hypothesis and law C — at L = 3, 4 and 5. Adding a
   full round of depth-1 case analysis (assert one literal, close, look for a contradiction:
   a hand proof by contradiction of depth one, at every vertex and type) adds **nothing**: no
   literal fails. So no amount of one-hexagon reasoning, with or without single-vertex case
   splits, reaches (V).
4. **No proof by counting, even with non-negativity (computed, exact; `h121`–`h123`).** This
   is the main negative result and it closes the route the brief proposed. The type model's
   equations are one-hot Σ_a t(v,a) = 1, (T2) Σ_{v∈h} t(v,α_h(v)) = 2, and the six cage
   literals; every law of the project (Pˣ, Pʸ, Pᶻ, C, (P), Lemma C, the grid counts) is a
   rational combination of them. A proof of the form *"take a rational combination of the
   laws and read off a sum of non-negative indicators"* — the shape of Lemma S1, and of every
   sum round the x-circle the brief suggested — exists **iff** the LP
   max { Σ_{b ∈ B-grid g+1} t(b,x) : equations, t ≥ 0 } is 0. It is not:

   | L | B-sites on grid g+1 | LP optimum | verified exactly |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 3 | 18 | 26/3 ≈ 8.67 | yes |
   | 4 | 32 | 178/9 ≈ 19.78 | yes |
   | 5 | 50 | 299/9 ≈ 33.22 | yes |

   "Verified exactly" means `h123_lpcert.py` rationalises the LP optimum and checks with
   `Fraction` arithmetic that it satisfies **every** equation exactly and has no negative
   entry, so the verdict does not rest on floating point. Adding every cage-local clause of
   length ≤ 3 (136 per cage kind), on every one of the 216 cages — 29 376 extra inequalities at
   L = 3 — leaves the optimum at 26/3 unchanged (`h122`). So finite case analysis inside one
   cage does not help the counting route either.
5. **What closes the gap is one sixth of the integrality (solver, L = 3, 4, 5; `h124`).**
   Impose 0/1 on the indicators of **one** (axis, sublattice) family only — for example
   ξ = [· x] on A, 108 of the 648 variables at L = 3 — and leave all the others merely
   non-negative. The MILP optimum is then 0. This holds for each of the six families
   (xA, xB, yA, zA, yB, zB tested). So

     **(V) = [the linear laws] + [t ≥ 0] + [t(·, k) ∈ {0,1} on one sublattice].**

   That is the exact residual, and it is the sheet theorem's shape (f ∈ {0,1} for one field),
   not Lemma S1's.
6. **(V) needs the transverse torus as well as the x-circle (computed, `h100`).** The orient
   report records the x-slab threshold |dx| ≤ 2L−2 with the full transverse torus. Holding
   that threshold and shrinking the transverse window by **one plane** destroys the forcing
   completely: at L = 4 with RX = 6 = 2L−2, the backbone forces 368 literals at RT = 8 (the
   whole transverse torus) and exactly 22 — the hypothesis and law C, nothing else — at
   RT = 7 and RT = 6. At L = 6 with RX = 5 the same happens at RT = 12 (38 forced) versus
   RT = 9, 10, 11 (22 forced). So (V) is a statement about all three circles, not a "light
   cone along x"; the light cone of the orient report §4 only exists once the transverse
   torus has closed.

## Setting and coordinates

The type-only model: one-hot types plus (T2) ("every hexagon has exactly two matches"), no
bonds, no sign field, no ice rule. By the cage theorem (laws report), (T2) is equivalent,
given one-hot, to C ∧ Pˣ ∧ Pʸ ∧ Pᶻ on every cage of both kinds.

Put the A-vertex v at the origin. Then, with d₀ = (1,1,1), d₁ = (1,−1,−1), d₂ = (−1,1,−1),
d₃ = (−1,−1,1):

| object | position | type under the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| hole O of the A-cage | (2,0,0) | — |
| rod v = w_x^− | (0,0,0) | x |
| rod v′ = w_x^+ | (4,0,0) | x |
| lower bridgehead β = v + d₀ | (1,1,1) | y |
| lower bridgehead β′ = v + d₁ | (1,−1,−1) | y |
| upper bridgehead γ = v′ + d₂ | (3,1,−1) | z |
| upper bridgehead γ′ = v′ + d₃ | (3,−1,1) | z |
| transverse methylenes w_y^± | (2,±2,0) | ? |
| transverse methylenes w_z^± | (2,0,±2) | ? |

Grids: g = 0 is v's A-grid, g+1 = 1 is β's B-grid, g+3 = 3 is γ's, g+4 = 4 is v′'s.
The map (x,y,z) ↦ (x,−y,−z) is a lattice symmetry (it permutes d₀ ↔ d₁, d₂ ↔ d₃, fixes every
axis label and fixes A) and it fixes the hypothesis, exchanging β ↔ β′ and γ ↔ γ′. Everything
below therefore comes in mirror pairs.

**(V)** is: every B-site of grid g+1 is type y — equivalently (given Lemma S1 and the
grid-count identity) none is type x. Everything printed in this section is checked by
`h125_anatomy.py`, which derives the cage, its hexagons and their axes from the lattice
itself.

## Lemma U1 (proved): the four hexagons of the cage, and law C

**Lemma U1.** *Under the hypothesis, the four transverse methylenes are unmatched:
w_y^± is not type y and w_z^± is not type z. Nothing else follows from the cage.*

The cage's four hexagons, each written as a cycle with the hexagon axis at each vertex
(`h125_anatomy.py 4`; three bridgeheads and three methylenes apiece, as Lemma 1 of the laws
report requires):

| hexagon | cycle (axis at each vertex) |
|---|---|
| H₁ | v (x) — β (y) — w_z^+ (z) — γ′ (x) — w_y^− (y) — β′ (z) |
| H₂ | v (x) — β (z) — w_y^+ (y) — γ (x) — w_z^− (z) — β′ (y) |
| H₃ | w_z^+ (z) — γ′ (y) — v′ (x) — γ (z) — w_y^+ (y) — β (x) |
| H₄ | v′ (x) — γ (y) — w_z^− (z) — β′ (x) — w_y^− (y) — γ′ (z) |

*Proof.* Each bridgehead occurs in three of the four hexagons, with the three different axes
(laws, Lemma 1(b)); each methylene w_k^σ occurs in two, always with axis k (Lemma 1(a)). Read
off the matches:

- H₁ and H₂ contain v with axis x: v is type x, so it matches in both. H₃ and H₄ contain v′
  with axis x, likewise.
- In H₁ the bridgeheads are β (axis y), γ′ (axis x), β′ (axis z). Under yy/zz exactly one of
  them matches, namely β. In H₂ they are β (z), γ (x), β′ (y): exactly β′ matches. In H₃ they
  are γ′ (y), γ (z), β (x): exactly γ. In H₄ they are γ (y), β′ (x), γ′ (z): exactly γ′.

So each hexagon already has two matches — one rod, one bridgehead — and (T2) says its two
remaining vertices, which are transverse methylenes carrying their own axis, do not match:

- H₁: w_z^+ not z, w_y^− not y;  H₂: w_y^+ not y, w_z^− not z;
- H₃: w_z^+ not z, w_y^+ not y;  H₄: w_z^− not z, w_y^− not y.

Together these are the four exclusions. They are exactly law C at this cage: C says exactly
two of the six methylenes are matched, and the two rods already are. ∎

Two remarks worth keeping.

- **The hypothesis is tight for (T2) on the cage.** Under yy/zz every hexagon of the cage has
  its match quota filled by the hypothesis alone. That is the structural reason this pattern
  is special, and it is why it propagates nothing locally: a saturated hexagon constrains
  only its own remaining vertices.
- **The four exclusions are all the cage gives.** The type-only closure of the whole torus
  under one-hexagon reasoning stops here (verdict 3).

## Lemma U2 (proved; computed): the neighbouring cages

Take the B-cages centred at the B-holes of grid g+1 adjacent to β or β′. There are six such
holes, and exactly two are adjacent to **both** β and β′: O₁ = (1,1,−1) and its mirror
O₂ = (1,−1,1). Those two carry three of the four constrained bridgeheads as methylenes; the
other four carry only one. At O₁ (`h125_anatomy.py`):

| role | site | known |
|---|---|---|
| w_x^− | A₁ = (−1,1,−1) | ? (B-site on grid g−1) |
| w_x^+ | γ = (3,1,−1) | z |
| w_y^− | β′ = (1,−1,−1) | y |
| w_y^+ | A₂ = (1,3,−1) | ? |
| w_z^− | A₃ = (1,1,−3) | ? |
| w_z^+ | β = (1,1,1) | y |
| bridgeheads | b₁ = (2,2,0) = w_y^+ of the A-cage, b₂ = (2,0,−2) = w_z^− of the A-cage, b₃ = (0,2,−2), v | b₁ not y, b₂ not z, v is x |

So two of the B-cage's four bridgeheads are transverse methylenes of the A-cage, a third is a
new site b₃, and the fourth is v itself.

**Lemma U2.** *One of the four hexagons of the B-cage at O₁ is H₂, a hexagon of the A-cage.
The other three give*

  (K2)  [b₁ x] + [A₂ y] + [b₃ z] + [A₁ x] = 2
  (K3)  [b₂ x] + [A₃ z] + [b₃ y] + [A₁ x] = 1
  (K4)  [b₁ z] + [b₂ y] + [A₃ z] + [b₃ x] + [A₂ y] = 2

*and law C at O₁ reads [A₁ x] + [A₂ y] + [A₃ z] = 1. None of these forces a literal, and the
three together force none: every literal appearing in them is realised in some (T2) state
with the cage.*

*Proof.* The three hexagons are, with their axes,

- v (y) — β (z) — b₁ (x) — A₂ (y) — b₃ (z) — A₁ (x),
- v (z) — β′ (y) — b₂ (x) — A₃ (z) — b₃ (y) — A₁ (x),
- b₁ (z) — γ (x) — b₂ (y) — A₃ (z) — b₃ (x) — A₂ (y).

v is type x and β type y, so neither matches in the first, whence (K2). In the second, β′ is
type y on axis y and matches, v (axis z) does not, whence (K3). In the third, γ is type z on
axis x and does not match, whence (K4). For law C at O₁: of the three known methylenes,
β = w_z^+ is type y and unmatched, γ = w_x^+ is type z and unmatched, and β′ = w_y^− is type y
and **matched**; C's quota of two matched methylenes therefore leaves exactly one match among
A₁, A₂ and A₃ on their own axes. The non-forcing is the closure computation of verdict 3. ∎

Note (K3): exactly one of four indicators. That is the strongest of the three, and it is still
a four-term disjunction. Combining (K2) and (K3) on their shared term [A₁ x] and splitting on
it produces two consistent branches, so not even a depth-one case analysis closes. The mirror
cage at O₂ gives the mirror equations under (x,y,z) ↦ (x,−y,−z), with γ′ in place of γ.

**The A-cages at O ± 4e_x give nothing either.** Their only contact with the hypothesis is one
rod: the cage at O + 4e_x = (6,0,0) has v′ as its lower methylene and the pair (5,±1,±1) as
its lower bridgeheads, of which nothing is known. Its Pˣ law reads
[v″ x] = 1 + (#type-x upper bridgeheads) − (#type-x lower bridgeheads) with v″ = (8,0,0), and
both counts are free in {0,1,2}. This is why the backbone fact "the whole A x-line through v
is type x" is itself x-global, not a step-by-step propagation.

## The grid-count sums (proved): both suggested sums are 0 = 0

The brief asked for the sum of Pˣ over the B-cages of one x-line of B-holes, and over all
cages of one grid. Both are identities with no content, and the reason is worth recording.

Write Pˣ at a cage in neighbour form (laws, Task 4): the upper bridgeheads of the cage whose
upper x-middle is M are M's two neighbours one step down in x, and the lower bridgeheads are
the two neighbours of m = M − 4e_x one step up. So Pˣ is

  ξ(M) − ξ(m) = D(M) − U(m),

with D(M) the number of type-x neighbours of M one step down in x and U(m) the number one step
up from m.

- **Over one x-line of cages.** The cages along a line have M_t = m_{t+1}, so the left side
  telescopes round the x-circle to 0. The right side is Σ_t [D(M_t) − U(M_{t−1})], a sum of
  the same shape; it is the sum of (P) along the line and is 0 by the same telescoping. No
  term survives.
- **Over all the cages of one grid.** Let the cages have their holes on grid G, so m lies on
  G−2 and M on G+2, the lower bridgeheads on G−1 and the upper on G+1. Each B-site on G+1 has
  two A-neighbours on G+2, each of which is the M of exactly one cage of grid G, so it is an
  upper bridgehead of exactly two of them; likewise below. Each A-site on G±2 is the M or m of
  exactly one cage. Summing Pˣ therefore gives

    2 n_B^x(G+1) − 2 n_B^x(G−1) = n_A^x(G+2) − n_A^x(G−2),

  which is the grid-count identity itself (both sides are 0 by Lemma C of sheetcurl read along
  x). The same computation with law C's c-form over a grid gives
  2(n_A^x(G−2) + n_A^x(G+2)) − 4 n_A^x(G) = 0, again the grid-count identity.

The hypothesis lives at one cage; any sum over a whole grid or a whole line averages it away.
This is not a defect of the particular sums chosen: **no** weighting works, by verdict 4.

What the grid-count identity does do is the step *after* (V): n_B^x is the same on every
B-grid, so (V) gives n_B^x = 0 everywhere, hence (by Lemma S1 and non-negativity) B is x-free,
and the sublattice mirror of M′ gives curl-free. That half was already proved.

## Why counting stops: the LP relaxation (computed, exact)

**The equivalence.** Let A t = b be the system {one-hot, (T2), the six cage literals} in the
648 (L = 3) type variables, and let the target be σ(t) = Σ_{b ∈ B-grid g+1} t(b,x). Suppose a
hand proof of (V) has the form: *take rational multipliers y for the equations; the resulting
functional yᵀA t = yᵀb has every coefficient ≥ the corresponding coefficient of σ, and
yᵀb ≤ 0; since t ≥ 0, σ(t) ≤ yᵀA t = yᵀb ≤ 0, so every term of σ vanishes.* That is exactly
Lemma S1's argument, exactly the sheet-disjunction argument's linear half, and exactly what
"sum a P law or law C round the x-circle and read off a non-negative count" means. By LP
duality such a y exists **iff** max { σ(t) : A t = b, t ≥ 0 } = 0. (Upper bounds t ≤ 1 are
implied by one-hot and t ≥ 0, so nothing is lost by omitting them.)

**The verdict.** The maximum is 26/3 at L = 3, 178/9 at L = 4 and 299/9 at L = 5 — between 48 %
and 66 % of the grid. `h123_lpcert.py` rationalises the optimal point and verifies with exact
`Fraction` arithmetic that all 654 (L = 3) equations hold exactly and no coordinate is
negative. So the witness is a genuine rational point of the polyhedron, and the negative
result is **computed, not solver**.

**Cuts do not help.** `h122_lpcuts.py` enumerates the 1200 (T2)-feasible assignments of a cage
of each kind and extracts every minimal partial assignment of at most three of its ten
vertices that no feasible assignment extends: 30 of length ≤ 2 and 136 of length ≤ 3, per
kind. Imposed as linear cuts on **every** one of the 216 cages (6 480 and 29 376 inequalities
at L = 3), they leave the optimum at 26/3 exactly. So "case analysis inside a cage, then sum"
is also dead.

**What the LP does prove.** Maximising and minimising each variable separately (`h122 forced`,
L = 3) pins 32 non-types and 6 types. The 6 types are the hypothesis itself: the LP forces no
new positive type anywhere. Of the 32 exclusions, 16 are the hypothesis's one-hot companions
(12) and law C (4). The other 16 are new: eight at B-sites, on the grids dx = ±1 and dx = ±5,
and eight at A-sites, on dx = ±4 and on the antipodal grid dx = 6. Every one of them is
extremal — the B-sites all have a transverse coordinate of magnitude 5, the largest a B-site
can have at L = 3, and the A-sites are either on the antipodal x-grid or at transverse
offset 6, the antipodal transverse plane. Not one is near the cage. They are part of the
spread the orient report saw, and are already torus-global statements themselves; so the LP
reproduces some of the light cone and cannot close it.

transverse lattice in every (T2) state, and 0 ≤ N_B ≤ L. A hand proof of (V) should be
looked for as a statement about that separable integer function under the hypothesis.

## Branching certificate (follow-up)

Verdict 5 says (V) follows from the laws, non-negativity, and 0/1 on one (axis, sublattice)
family. That makes a finite case split possible in principle: a set S of that family's
variables such that **every** 0/1 assignment of S closes the LP (optimum 0, or infeasible).
Call such an S a *uniform certificate*; it is a hand proof by case analysis on |S| bits, one
Farkas certificate per branch.

**How S is tested (proved).** Fix any order on S and run a depth-first prefix search, pruning
at a node whose LP already closes. Adding further fixed values to a closed LP keeps it closed
(extra bounds can only lower an optimum, and an infeasible system stays infeasible), so if
every leaf closes then every full assignment of S passes through a closed node; and an open
leaf *is* an assignment of S that does not close. The verdict is therefore exact and does not
depend on the order, and the variables the search actually branched on form a smaller uniform
certificate. That is `h127_grow.py`.

### Lemma U0 (proved; computed): four literals are the whole hypothesis

**Lemma U0.** *Given one-hot and (T2), "v and v′ are type x, one lower bridgehead is type y
and one upper bridgehead is type z" forces the other lower bridgehead to be type y and the
other upper bridgehead to be type z.*

*Proof.* Say β and γ are the named ones, and use the four hexagons of Lemma U1.

- H₁ = v (x) — β (y) — w_z^+ (z) — γ′ (x) — w_y^− (y) — β′ (z). Here v and β match, so the
  other four do not: in particular **β′ is not type z**, hence β′ ∈ {x, y}.
- H₃ = w_z^+ (z) — γ′ (y) — v′ (x) — γ (z) — w_y^+ (y) — β (x). Here v′ and γ match, so
  **γ′ is not type y** (hence γ′ ∈ {x, z}) and w_y^+ is not type y.
- H₂ = v (x) — β (z) — w_y^+ (y) — γ (x) — w_z^− (z) — β′ (y). v matches; β is type y so it
  does not; γ is type z so it does not; w_y^+ is not type y. So exactly one of [w_z^− z] and
  [β′ y] holds.
- H₄ = v′ (x) — γ (y) — w_z^− (z) — β′ (x) — w_y^− (y) — γ′ (z). v′ matches; γ is type z so
  it does not; w_y^− is not type y (from H₁). So exactly one of [w_z^− z], [β′ x], [γ′ z]
  holds.

If β′ were type x, then [β′ y] = 0, so H₂ gives [w_z^− z] = 1, and H₄ would carry two matches
among its three free terms. So **β′ is type y**, [w_z^− z] = 0, and H₄ then gives
**γ′ is type z**. ∎

**Computed** (`h120_uhand.py` closure, L = 3, 4, 5, all four choices of which lower and which
upper bridgehead is named): the one-hexagon closure forces the other two in every case, and
determines exactly the same ten sites as yy/zz does. So Theorem U′'s four literals and yy/zz
are the same hypothesis in the type-only model, and every number below is unchanged by using
either — `h126_branch.py milp` returns identical optima and identical node counts (7 at L = 3,
37 at L = 4) for `yyzz`, `u4` and `u4b`.

12 — but it branches on **65 distinct A-sites**, so its tree is not a uniform certificate.

### The uniform certificates (computed)

`h127_grow.py growshrink` grows S greedily — at each step it adds the family variable that
minimises the worst remaining open branch, choosing from the ten or twelve most fractional
candidates — and then deletes elements one at a time while the prefix tree still closes. (The
`--prune` variant, which instead maximises the number of branches closed, was tried only for
the L = 5 attempt and did not help.)  Greedy gives an upper bound on the minimum |S|.

The L = 3 certificate, in coordinates relative to v (so that the cage's own sites are the ones
of the setting table):

  S = { w_y^+ = (2,2,0), w_y^− = (2,−2,0), w_z^− = (2,0,−2),
        v + 4e_z = (0,0,4), v′ + 4e_z = (4,0,4), (−4,−2,2) }.

Three of the six are the cage's own transverse methylenes; two are the 4e_z translates of the
two rods; one is four grids away. The L = 4 certificate is

    (−6,0,6), (−2,−6,0), (−6,−6,−4) },

with only two cage sites, and it touches six of the eight A-grids.

### The structure of S (computed)

- **It is not the x-line.** Fixing the whole A x-line through v — the set the backbone makes
  monochromatic — leaves the worst branch at 8.67 (L = 3), 19.50 (L = 4), 33.00 (L = 5), that
  is, almost the unbranched LP optimum. The line is worth nothing.
- **It is not a grid or a chain.** The xB certificate at L = 3 meets all six B-grids
  (4, 2, 5, 4, 5, 2 sites) and its transverse positions run over the whole transverse torus.
- **It is not translation-stable.** The natural structured guess suggested by the L = 3
  answer — the four transverse methylenes together with the 4e_z and 4e_y translates of the
  two rods, {(2,±2,0), (2,0,±2), (0,0,4), (4,0,4), (0,4,0), (4,4,0)} — **is** a uniform
  certificate at L = 3 (30 closed nodes) and **is not** at L = 4. So S is not one fixed local pattern
  repeated; the answer at each L is a different scattered set.
- **It grows with L.** 6 → 9 for the A x-family from L = 3 to L = 4 (both deletion-minimal
  under the greedy construction, so these are upper bounds on the true minimum). The greedy
  curve at L = 5 was still at 31.4 of 33.2 after four variables and did not finish in budget,
  so |S| there is certainly larger again.

### The per-branch certificates (computed)

For the six-variable L = 3 certificate the prefix tree has 29 closed nodes: **18 close by LP
infeasibility and 11 by an optimum of 0**. Sizing them (`h128_certduals.py`, `h129_extent.py`):

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| (T2) rows with non-zero weight in the LP dual (of 432) | min 233, median 313, max 324 |
| one-hot rows with non-zero weight (of 216) | min 170, median 197, max 205 |
| the same after L1-minimising the certificate over all 29 nodes | min **25**, median 113, max 359 |
| x-grids touched by the hexagons used | all 12, in every node |
| smallest x-slab \|dx\| ≤ R that already closes the node | R = 3 for 6 nodes, 4 for 4, 5 for 4, the whole torus for 15 |

The dual of a degenerate LP is not unique, so the first rows are only an upper bound on how
big a certificate must be; the L1-minimised row is the honest one. It says that the sparsest
combination is 25 hexagon laws for the easiest branch and around 113 for a typical one. The
xB certificate is worse still: 113 of its 114 nodes close by infeasibility, and the one LP
dual uses 316 of 432 (T2) rows across all 12 grids.

### Verdict on the branching route

The case split is small — six bits and twenty-nine branches at L = 3 — and that is genuinely
encouraging. What is not encouraging is what closes each branch: a combination of the order
of a hundred (T2) laws spread over every grid of the torus, dense in the cage basis too. So
the branching certificate does **not** by itself turn into a hand proof; it turns the problem
into "describe, uniformly in L, the Farkas certificate of each of the branches", which is the
same globality that verdict 4 identified, now packaged 29 times over.

Two things from this are worth carrying forward. **Lemma U0** is a real simplification of the
hypothesis: only four literals are needed, and the proof is four hexagons. And the branch that
closes with 25 laws is the natural place to start reading a certificate by hand; if its
structure is recognisable, the others may follow the same shape.

## Residual and next targets

What is proved: Lemma U1, Lemma U2, the two grid sums, and the equivalence "a counting proof
exists iff the LP optimum is 0". What is not: (V), and with it Theorem U.

The residual is now exact:

> **Residual (V′).** Let τ be one-hot with (T2) on the torus, carrying the yy/zz cage at O.
> Suppose in addition that the indicator [· x] on A takes values in {0,1} — which it does —
> and that everything else is only known to be non-negative and to satisfy the laws. Show
> Σ_{b ∈ B-grid g+1} [b x] = 0.

That is a genuinely smaller problem than (V): the continuous relaxation of five sixths of the
variables is already enough, so the argument has to look only at one sublattice's x-field
together with linear algebra. Three concrete next steps:

1. **Find the certificate, case by case.** For a *fixed* integral ξ on A, (V) is a pure
   Farkas question in the remaining variables. Compute, for the integral ξ patterns that
   survive the hypothesis, the certificate that the solver is implicitly using, and look for
   a uniform description. The x-line backbone fact (ξ ≡ 1 on v's line) should be proved first
   in this form, since it is the same kind of statement one dimension smaller.
2. **Use the separable column count.** N_B(i,j) = F(i) + G(j) with 0 ≤ N_B ≤ L is the one
   integer-plus-linear object already proved and already global in all three circles.
   (V) says N_B ≡ 0. The hypothesis has to be turned into a bound on F or G.
3. **Compute the deletion-minimal core of (V)**, still not done. Given verdict 6 it should be
   sought as a set of hexagons meeting every transverse plane, not as a slab.
4. **Read one branch certificate.** The branching section below gives a six-variable case
   split at L = 3 whose easiest branch closes with 25 (T2) laws after L1-minimisation. That
   is the smallest completely explicit object the problem has produced; if its shape is
   recognisable the other 28 branches may follow it.

A warning for whoever takes it up: the LP witnesses of `h123` are the reason to stop looking
for a weighted sum of laws. Any proposed proof that never uses an indicator's integrality is
refuted by those points, which satisfy every law and every non-negativity and have two thirds
of the grid type x.
