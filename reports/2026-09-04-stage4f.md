## 1. What was implemented

### The panel change (additive, `icemix/panel.py`)

`PanelSpec.helicity` (default **False**) adds four even-parity families
computed from the polarisation components `measure_panel` already records:

| family | components | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `helicity_h1` | 3 (one per q axis) | `h_k(q_min) = Im(P_i(q_min)* P_j(q_min))`, `(i,j,k)` cyclic |
| `helicity_total_h1` | 1 | `h_x + h_y + h_z` at `q_min` |
| `helicity_h2` | 3 | the same at `2 q_min` |
| `helicity_total_h2` | 1 | the sum at `2 q_min` |

`h_k = Re P_i · Im P_j − Im P_i · Re P_j` — three multiplies and a
subtraction on the float64 group-count values inside the sampling loop, one
per axis per harmonic per sample. Both `P_i` and `P_j` are transverse to `q`
by construction (asserted in `build_panel`). Sign and normalisation match
`experiments/05_thin_even/run_thin_even.py` exactly; the run script's
`helicity_from_polarisation` is the single shared definition and
`tests/test_stage4f.py` asserts the panel's grouped-count route agrees with
it to 1e-9 relative on four independent lanes of a cubic L = 2 state.

`icemix.panel.helicity_ratio(panel)` computes `R_h` per family and picks the
best resolved `q_min` member (`max` over `R_h`, which is the conservative
choice for the FAIL-UP reading).

### The change does not touch the existing panel

Verified three ways, all in `tests/test_stage4f.py`:

1. **Against the pre-change module.** `git show HEAD:icemix/panel.py` was
   materialised as a temporary module and run on a short deterministic panel
   at `(1,2,2)` (1 block, 300 samples, seed 777). Every family's
   `tau_exp`, `tau_int` and `rho_head` from the current module is
   **bit-identical** to it; those numbers are frozen in the test as
   `GOLDEN_PRE_CHANGE`. The same check was run at cubic `(2,2,2)` with 400
   samples during development and also came back bit-identical.
2. **helicity on vs off.** With `helicity=True` the seven stage-3 families
   return the same `tau_exp`, `tau_int`, their errors and their whole
   `rho_head`, digit for digit, and the sampling interval and burn-in are
   unchanged — the observable is a function of numbers already in hand and
   consumes no RNG.
3. **Against stage 3 itself.** The cubic ladder was run with the *very*
   `tau_guess_sweeps` values stage 3 used (read out of
   `experiments/03_campaign/results/results.json`), the same seeds
   (`1000 + 7L`), block counts, sample counts, `per_tau`, `burn_taus` and
   fast-pass lengths. `tau_exp(P_transverse_h1)` reproduces the stage-3
   table to 1e-6 relative or better at every size — and that residual is the
   rounding of the six-significant-figure stage-3 constants I compared
   against, not a difference in the chain (see §5).

### Stochastic — the campaign estimator, from the reference state R

512 replicas in 8 blocks, 20,000 samples at 39 attempts (2,000 relaxation
times per replica), burn-in 19,497 attempts, `giant-start-1`, finite-series
bias correction applied — i.e. the `taurun` path the campaign uses.

| observable | MC `tau_exp` (att) | exact, *same estimator* (att) | rel. error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `helicity_yz` | **378.08 ± 1.92** | 377.55 | **+0.14 %** |
| `P⊥(q_min)` | **211.02 ± 0.25** | 211.60 | **−0.27 %** |
| **`R_h`** | **1.7917 ± 0.0094** | **1.7843** | **+0.41 %** |

**Reading (v) passes, and the caveat matters.** The registered value 1.61 is
the *asymptotic* ratio `−1/log λ` of the slowest eigenvalue each observable
couples to. The stochastic estimator does not measure that: it fits the
log-slope of `ρ(t)` over the window `ρ ∈ [0.02, 0.6]`, which is
amplitude-weighted and therefore lands lower for both observables and higher
for their ratio. Applying the *identical* fit to the *exact* `ρ(t)`,
subsampled at the same 39-attempt interval, gives 1.7843 — and the Monte
Carlo returns 1.7917 ± 0.0094, agreeing to 0.4 %. So the estimator is
faithful; the 1.61 → 1.78 shift is the estimator's definition, not a defect,
and it is measured rather than asserted.

The substantive half of the reading is unambiguous either way: **the
stochastic estimator sees the helicity mode at (1,2,2)**, returning
`R_h` ≈ 1.79 ≫ 1 — the helicity relaxing far *slower* than the
polarisation — where every stage-3 panel member is exactly orthogonal to
that mode by symmetry. Had the implementation been wrong, this is the number
that would have come back at or below 1.

Run through the **production panel** instead (`q` on the two thick axes,
512 replicas, 6,000 samples), `(1,2,2)` now returns headline family
`helicity_total_h1` (even) with `R_h` = 1.8986. The panel's headline is no
longer blind to the chain's slowest mode at this cell.

## 3. R-parity of the helicity members, verified numerically

Registered as a check rather than assumed from the algebra.

- **Exhaustively** on all 221,424 states of the `(1,2,2)` giant component:
  every helicity column satisfies `h(R s) = h(s)` to a relative deviation
  below 1e-12 (they are **even**), and every non-vanishing polarisation
  column satisfies `P(R s) = −P(s)` to the same tolerance (**odd**). The
  longitudinal `P_k(q_k)` columns are identically zero by the lattice
  divergence-free constraint — float noise at 1e-17, carrying no parity —
  and are classified `null` rather than forced into a verdict.
- **On sampled accessible states of cubic cells** L = 2 and L = 3 (6 states
  each, taken from the engine after burn-in, each compared with its bitwise
  complement): helicity even, polarisation odd, to the same tolerance.

Verdict: helicity even everywhere. This is what makes the family a genuine
complement to `P⊥` rather than a re-measurement of it.

### 5.5 What this says physically

Two independent even bilinears in `P` — the scalar `|P⊥(q_min)|²` and the
pseudoscalar `h_k(q_min)` — both converge to exactly half the polarisation's
relaxation time, from opposite sides, by L = 8. That is the Wick/two-photon
prediction of the plan's §4 met by a channel the plan did not have, and it
is stronger evidence than the `|P⊥|²` check alone: `|P⊥|²` at `τ/2` is
partly a Gaussian identity, whereas the helicity is a *different*
representation of the cubic point group (it changes sign under every
improper operation) and had no obligation to land on the same number.

The `(1,2,2)` anomaly is therefore confirmed to be what stage 4d §6
suspected: a property of cells with a two-dimensional cross-section and only
one cell of thickness, not of the Coulomb phase. In cubic geometry the
long-wavelength helicity is an ordinary two-photon composite and is *faster*
than the polarisation, so the panel's headline — and every "lower bound on
1/Δ" statement in the note that rests on it — is unaffected.

## 7. Defects and deviations recorded

1. **`pyrun.sh` is unusable in this repository**, as in stage 4d: there is
   no `.venv/`. Everything ran under
   `~/.pyenv/versions/3.12.13/bin/python3` (numpy 1.26.4,
   scipy 1.17.1), as the brief allowed.
2. **`exptop` is not importable from that interpreter.** The run appends
   `~/repo/project/exptop/src` to `sys.path` and uses the
   **canonical** `exptop.progress.ProgressReporter` — the stage-4d
   arrangement, copied verbatim. `grep -rl "class ProgressReporter"
   --include='*.py' . | grep -v .venv` over this repository returns
   **nothing**: no vendored reporter existed and none was created.
   `results.json` records which reporter it got
   (`meta.canonical_progress_feed = "canonical"`). The repo-local heartbeat
   runs alongside it with per-writer temp filenames and non-fatal I/O.
3. **A near-zero column has no parity, and the first version of the
   R-parity check said so wrongly.** `P_k(q_k)` (longitudinal) is
   identically zero by the divergence-free constraint, so `max|v[perm]+v| /
   max|v|` is a ratio of two float-noise quantities and came out ~1.3
   instead of ~0. Caught by the test suite before the production run;
   fixed by scaling each column against the largest member of its family
   and classifying a numerically-zero column `null`. No result depended on
   it, but it would have made `all_polarisation_odd` read `false` for a
   reason that is not physics.
4. **`tau_exp` fit windows make the (1,2,2) ratio 1.78, not 1.61.**
   Recorded here rather than buried: the registered reading's 1.61 is an
   asymptotic eigenvalue ratio and the estimator measures a windowed
   log-slope. The report quotes both, and the pass criterion compares the
   Monte Carlo against the *exact answer under the identical estimator*
   (1.7843), which is the only like-for-like comparison. Anyone re-reading
   the plan's reading (v) should read it as "the estimator reproduces the
   exact ratio it is capable of measuring", which it does to 0.4 %.
5. **The machine was contended throughout.** The stage-4a/4b sweep from
   another session (started 10:43) and this task's own test suite ran
   alongside; walls are 3.5–4.5× the stage-3 figures and the L = 16
   projection of 1,048 s is running long. This affects timings only — the
   chain, the seeds and every τ are contention-independent, which is why
   the stage-3 reproduction is exact.
6. **Serialisation was honoured.** No Markov-chain measurement was launched
