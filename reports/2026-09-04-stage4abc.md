### 2.2 The trap that had to be disarmed first: the divisor artefact

The support of `N` is **not** all of `Z³`. Computed from the histogram (not
assumed): the support is a coset of a sublattice `L ⊂ Z³` of **index 8** at
every cell, with basis in row-echelon form

| cell | support-lattice basis | index |
|---|---|---|
| (1,1,1) | (2,2,0), (0,2,2), (0,0,2) | 8 |
| (1,1,2), (1,1,3), (1,1,4) | (2,2,0), (0,2,0), (0,0,2) | 8 |
| (1,2,2), (1,2,3) | (2,0,0), (0,2,0), (0,0,2) | 8 |

So `W ± e_j` is simply **not a lattice point**: the literal reading
"`N(W)² ≥ N(W−e_j)N(W+e_j)`" is satisfied vacuously (`N = 0` on both sides)
and every axis line would appear riddled with "internal zeros". Reporting a
PASS on that basis would be worthless and reporting a FAIL on the internal
zeros would be a fabricated result. `icemix.logconcave` therefore computes the
support lattice by integer row reduction and, for each direction `v`, the
least positive `t` with `t·v ∈ L` (the **primitive lattice vector** in that
direction); every line test below is on those points. The step is 2 for every
direction at every cell.

This is the one place where the registered wording and the lattice disagree,
and the resolution is recorded here rather than silently applied: **the
substantive reading is on the primitive lattice vector, and that is what
"PASS" below means.** The unrescaled reading is separately true and vacuous.

`tests/test_stage4abc.py` carries two negative controls
(`test_a_diagonal_violation_would_be_caught`,
`test_an_internal_zero_would_be_caught`) so the PASS is not a test that
cannot fire.

### 2.5 What a 4a PASS does and does not mean

It means the *joint* winding-sector counts on the fully periodic torus are
log-concave along every lattice line tested, in exact integer arithmetic, at
every cell reachable by the DP — including (1,2,3), a 96-spin cell with
3.08×10⁹ ice states that no enumeration can touch. That is the falsifier the
plan registered, and it did not fire.

It does **not** mean the generating polynomial is Lorentzian, and 4b now says
it is not even pairwise-negatively-correlatable in any diagonal gauge. The two
readings are independent: 4a is about the coefficient sequence of the flux
grading, 4b about the bond variables themselves.

## 3. Item 4b — gauge-signability of bond correlations

### 3.1 Method, and the two-method gate on every count

For each cell and each of the three measures, the exact integer statistics
`N`, `n_i = #{x_i = 1}`, `n_ij = #{x_i = x_j = 1}` were computed by two
independent routes and required to agree bit for bit:

* **route 1** — dense 0/1 matrix `X`, chunked `XᵀX` in float64 (every entry a
  non-negative integer below 2⁵³, with an assertion that the accumulation
  stayed exact and integral);
* **route 2** — one packed bitset per bond over the state index,
  `n_ij = popcount(b_i & b_j)`.

They agree everywhere (`pair_statistics` raises otherwise). The covariance is
the exact integer `C_ij = N·n_ij − n_i·n_j`, which is `N²·Cov(x_i, x_j)`.

The three measures: **A** uniform on the full ice manifold, **B** uniform on
`W = 0`, **C** uniform on the giant component of `W = 0`. Measure C is
skipped at (1,1,1), where the `W = 0` sector is wholly frozen (12 states, 12
singleton components) and there is no giant.

One exact structural check falls out and is confirmed at every cell and
measure: complementing every arrow maps the ice manifold to itself and sends
`W → −W`, so it fixes `W = 0` and (stage 1) permutes components with the
giant closed under it. Hence `n_i = N/2` for **every** bond — verified in
every row below.

### 3.3 Three independent decision procedures, and a hand-checkable certificate

The FAIL is not left resting on one implementation.

1. **Union–find with parity** on the constraint graph (edge for every nonzero
   `C_ij`, requiring `s_i s_j = −sign(C_ij)`): unsatisfiable.
2. **An odd-cycle certificate** from a BFS spanning forest — an independent
   traversal, not the union–find — extracted at every cell and measure. Every
   certificate found is a **triangle**.
3. **Exhaustive scan of all 2¹⁵ signings** at (1,1,1) (both measures): none
   works. This agrees with (1) and (2); the run asserts the agreement.

The (1,1,1) measure-A certificate, in full, so a reader can check it by hand
from `N = 90` and `n_i = 45`:

| pair | geometry | `n_ij` | `C = 90·n_ij − 45·45` | constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (15, 0) | share the B-site at (1,1,1) | 15 | 1350 − 2025 = **−675** | `s_15 = s_0` |
| (0, 1) | share the A-site at (0,0,0) | 15 | 1350 − 2025 = **−675** | `s_0 = s_1` |
| (1, 15) | share no site | 25 | 2250 − 2025 = **+225** | `s_1 ≠ s_15` |

The three constraints force `s_15 = s_0 = s_1` and `s_1 ≠ s_15`. **No sign
gauge exists, and the obstruction lives on three bonds of a 16-spin cell.**
The two negative constraints are the local 2-of-4 hypergeometric negativity at
the two shared sites; the positive one is a next-nearest bond pair. That is
the whole content of the FAIL: the dipolar sign structure of the ice
correlations produces an unbalanced triangle at the smallest possible scale,
so nothing about growing the cell can rescue it.

Every certificate found, at every cell and measure, is a **triangle** — the
obstruction is always three bonds, never a long cycle. In measure A they are
`[15, 0, 1]` at (1,1,1), `[31, 0, 1]` at (1,1,2), `[47, 0, 1]` at (1,1,3) and
`[63, 0, 1]` at (1,2,2); measures B and C give the same or a sibling triangle
(`[31, 0, 3]`, `[47, 0, 3]`). All eleven are in `results.json` under
`items.4b.cells.<cell>.measures.<M>.odd_cycle_certificate`, each with the
covariance sign and the required relation on every edge.

### 3.4 Nearest-neighbour sanity — and a correction to its premise

Two bonds meeting at a site are, in every state, two of the four incident bits
of which exactly two are set. The brief asked for this to be asserted as a
negative correlation. Result:

* **measures A and B: PASS at every cell** — all 48 / 96 / 144 / 192 site
  pairs strictly negative.
* **measure C at (1,1,2): FAIL.** Of the 96 site pairs on the 160-state giant
  component, 64 are negative and **32 are positive**, each with `C = +320`.
* measure C at (1,1,3) and (1,2,2): PASS.

This is not a bug, and it is worth stating precisely because the premise as
written is too strong. What the 2-of-4 constraint proves is a **sum rule**,
not a per-pair sign: at each site the six pair-products sum to 1 in every
state, so `Σ n_ij = N` over the six pairs, and with `n_i = N/2` throughout,
`Σ C_ij = N² − 6·N²/4 = −N²/2 < 0`. The *average* of the six is forced
negative; the individual signs are not. On the (1,1,2) giant that average is
realised as four pairs at `C = −3360` (`n_ij = 19`) and two at `C = +320`
(`n_ij = 42`); `4·19 + 2·42 = 160 = N`, exactly as the sum rule requires. The
full ice manifold has enough symmetry to make all six negative; the giant
component of a shattered quasi-1D cell does not. Stage 1 already flagged
(1,1,c) cells as pathological, and this is another instance.

Recorded, not hidden: the run records the offending pairs rather than
asserting and aborting, precisely so this could be reported.

### 3.5 Reading (iii) — not run, as registered

The registered trigger is "if (ii) PASSES at (1,1,1) or (1,1,2) in measure
(A)". It failed at both, so the strong-Rayleigh necessary condition under
conditioning was not run. The machinery exists and is tested
(`icemix.signgauge.conditional_violations`); a pre-flight at (1,1,1) with the
trivial all-`+1` gauge showed the full sweep of 98,280 (pair, conditioning)
triples costs 0.1 s, and the (1,1,2) sweep of 1.94×10⁶ triples ≈ 20 s, so cost
was never the obstacle — the trigger simply did not fire. This is recorded in
`results.json` under `items.4b.conditional_check`.

### 3.6 What the 4b FAIL settles

Per the registration, "a FAIL at (ii) closes the Lorentzian lead". It closes
it in the strongest available form: not "we could not find a gauge at the
sizes we could reach", but "no gauge exists, at the smallest cell, and here is
the three-bond reason". The §3 tempering in the plan anticipated exactly this
("the uniform ice measure has dipolar correlations whose sign depends on the
pair's orientation, so bond indicators are not pairwise negatively correlated
in the plain gauge") and asked whether a complement gauge rescues it, as it
did in the spin-ice repo. It does not, here.

none of these three numbers is a uniform-measure estimate, and the two
samplers disagree by more than the size dependence, so no rate should be read
off them.

designed: one loop reversal off the frozen Klein state leaves a median of 14

so these really are near-frozen starts — and all of them certify into the
giant.

### 4.3 Measured cost, against the pre-flight

The `L = 3` fresh sampler was the one pre-flight miss of this stage and it is
recorded as such: a 5-sample timing gave 0.87 s per `sample_w0_dfs`, but the
production mean over 3,000 starts is **3.32 s** — 3.8× the pre-flight, with a
sampler retry rate of **0.419 rejections per accepted start** (defect D2)
that the small sample had not seen. At the original 10,000-start target that
would have been a ~3 h phase against a 26 min projection. It was caught at
~600 starts by reading the live feed, the run was stopped, and `L = 3` was
resized to 3,000 fresh starts to fit the registered "~1 h per size" — 2,723 s
in the event. `L = 2` cost 314 s for 17,000 starts (mean sample 0.0018 s).

**Lesson recorded:** five samples were not enough to pre-flight a
rejection-sampling loop with a heavy tail. A pre-flight of a sampler that can
reject should time ≥100 units, not 5, and should report the rejection rate.

### 4.4 What 4c does and does not establish

It is 27,000 more consistent observations, at the two smallest cubic cells,
and no counterexample. It is **not** a proof, and the samplers' non-uniformity
means it is not even a bound on the density of a hypothetical non-frozen
non-giant component: such a component would have to be missed by both
samplers, and the loop sampler at `n_loops = 1` is precisely the probe
designed to find one. (NF⇒G) stands as a registered conjecture with
exhaustive support at (1,2,2) and 27,000/27,000 at `L = 2, 3`.

### 4.5 (1,2,3) exhaustive census — not attempted, with three blockers

1. **Hard representation blocker (D3).** (1,2,3) has 96 bonds.
   `enumerate_ice_states` raises for `n_bonds > 64`, and `build_flip_graph`
   / `components` / `verify_reference_in_giant` all assume a `uint64` state
   word. The census is not expensive-but-possible; it is impossible without
   a new state representation.
2. **Wall clock.** At the measured DFS leaf rate (≈1.0×10⁵ states/s from
   (1,1,4)'s 9,765,378 states in 98 s) the ≈3.08×10⁹-node tree projects to
   **≈8.5 h**, well over the 3 h rule.
3. **Memory.** 196,465,480 `W = 0` states as a Python list then a sorted
   `uint64` array is ≥16 GB before the graph, and the flip graph would carry
   ≈9.4×10⁹ edges (96 hexagons × 1.96×10⁸ states / 2) — far beyond what
   `scipy.sparse` can hold on a 121 GB machine.

The counts quoted (3,076,306,362 ice states; 196,465,480 at `W = 0`) are
stage 1's frontier-DP values, re-derived by this run's own DP at (1,2,3) and
agreeing exactly.

## 6. Defects recorded

**D1 — this project's progress feed is invisible to exptop, and `exptop` is
not importable from the interpreter the project uses.** `pyrun.sh info` fails
("no `.venv/` found"); the project runs on the pyenv 3.12.13 interpreter with
numpy 1.26.4 / scipy 1.17.1 / pytest 9.0.2 installed system-wide, and `exptop`
is not among them. Separately, `experiments/01_falsifiers/run_falsifiers.py`
(and its stage-2/3 successors) define a private `Progress` class writing
`results/progress.json` — a path **outside** the exptop feed root, so those
runs were never discoverable by `exptop`/`monitor.sh`. It is *not* a
`ProgressReporter` fork (the required grep finds no `class ProgressReporter`
anywhere in the repo), but it has the same operational consequence. The stage-4
run script uses the canonical `exptop.progress.ProgressReporter` and puts
`~/repo/project/exptop/src` on `sys.path` to reach it. **Suggested fix for the
main loop: `pip install -e ~/repo/project/exptop` into this interpreter (or
give the project a venv), then retire the three private `Progress` classes.**

**D2 — `icemix.start.sample_w0_dfs` raises on a non-negligible fraction of
seeds at cubic cells.** The randomised ice-rule DFS exhausts its 4×10⁶-node
budget (`RuntimeError: random_w0_state: node budget exceeded`), and the
subsequent loop drive can fail to reach `W = 0`. Measured rejection rate:
**0.000 per accepted start at `L = 2`** and **0.419 at `L = 3`** (recorded per
start in `results.json` as `sampler_attempts`, aggregated as
`sampler_retry_rate`). Stage 2's 780-start probe must have met the same
behaviour. The stage-4 run
wraps the call in a bounded retry (fresh RNG stream, ≤ 40 attempts) and
**records the retry count per start**, because the rejection is one more
source of non-uniformity in an already non-uniform sampler. Nothing in
`icemix` was changed.

**D3 — the enumeration and component machinery is hard-limited to 64 spins.**
`enumerate_ice_states` raises for `n_bonds > 64`, and `build_flip_graph`,
`components` and `verify_reference_in_giant` all assume a `uint64` state word
and `searchsorted` over a materialised array. This is why the (1,2,3)
exhaustive census (96 bonds) is not merely expensive but **impossible** with
the current representation, and why the 4a cross-check stops at (1,1,4). The
new `icemix/component.py` uses Python big integers and has no such limit, but
it closes one component at a time and is not a substitute for a full census.

**D4 (methodological, §2.2) — the winding support is a coset of an index-8
sublattice of `Z³`.** Any log-concavity reading stated on `W ± e_j` without
rescaling is vacuous, and any "internal zeros" reading stated that way is a
fabricated FAIL. Recorded here because the registration's wording does not
say it and a later reader could easily re-derive the wrong answer.

**D5 (§3.4) — "bonds sharing a site are negatively correlated" is not a
theorem.** The 2-of-4 constraint forces only the sum over the six pairs at a
site to be negative. The per-pair statement holds on the full manifold and on
`W = 0` but fails on the giant component of (1,1,2).

**D6 (mine, §5 r1) — a refactor broke the multiprocessing initialiser nine
minutes into the run.** Moving `bfs_component` out of the run script into
`icemix/component.py` dropped `hex_ops` from the imports, but `_c_init` still
called it; the dry run had passed *before* the refactor. `multiprocessing`
turns that into an infinite worker-respawn loop rather than a clean crash, so
the parent kept "running" and only the log showed the fault. Cost: the 4c
phase of r1. **Rule taken from it: re-run the dry run after every refactor,
however cosmetic, before a detached launch** — and, separately, a pool whose
initialiser fails should abort the run, not respawn forever.

**D7 (mine, §5) — four zombie feed cards.** Stopping a run with
`tmux kill-session` sends SIGHUP, which the canonical reporter does not handle
(it handles SIGTERM and SIGINT), so the card was never stamped terminal and
read as a hung job in exptop. Raised by the main loop; all four stamped
`failed` with notes and their stale `experiment.pid` files removed. Two fixes
adopted: `--no-feed` for sub-minute probes, and `kill -TERM` instead of
`tmux kill-session`. **A note for exptop itself: adding SIGHUP to the
reporter's handled signals would close this class of zombie card**, since
tmux/ssh teardown is the ordinary way a detached run dies.

**D8 (mine, §4.3) — a five-sample pre-flight of a rejection sampler.**
`sample_w0_dfs` at `L = 3` measured 0.87 s over 5 samples and 3.32 s over
3,000, because the small sample missed the retry tail (0.419 rejections per
accepted start). The projection was out by 3.8×. Caught from the live feed at
~600 starts, not from a log. **Rule: pre-flight a sampler that can reject with
≥100 units and report its rejection rate.**

## 8. Unexpected (not part of any registered reading)

1. **The winding support is a sublattice coset of index 8 at every cell**
   (§2.2). The registered log-concavity readings, taken literally on
   `W ± e_j`, are *vacuously* true, and the internal-zero reading taken
   literally would fail everywhere for a reason with no content. Anyone
   re-deriving 4a must rescale to the primitive lattice vector first. This is
   the single most likely way for a later reader to reach a wrong conclusion
   from the same data.
2. **The signability obstruction is local — three bonds — and it is local at
   every cell and every measure.** Every one of the eleven odd-cycle
   certificates is a triangle, and the (1,1,1) one is two site-sharing pairs
   (negative, from the 2-of-4 constraint) closed by one next-nearest pair
   (positive). Growing the cell cannot repair that: whatever else changes, a
   16-spin motif with an unbalanced triangle is present. This makes the 4b
   FAIL much stronger than "no gauge was found at the sizes we could reach".
3. **The tightest 4a margins are on the face diagonals of the two smallest
   3-D-ish cells** — discriminant 3 at (1,1,1) and 10 at (1,2,2), against
   10³–10⁷ elsewhere. If log-concavity fails anywhere on this lattice family,
   the face diagonals of small cells are where to look.
4. **Structural zero covariances appear only in the restricted measures.**
   The full ice manifold has no zero pair covariance at any cell; `W = 0` has
   128 at (1,1,2) and 384 at (1,1,3), and the (1,1,2) giant has 16. Fixing the
   flux (and then the component) creates exact independences that the
   unconstrained measure does not have.
5. **Nearest-neighbour negativity is not a theorem** and fails on the (1,1,2)
   giant: 32 of 96 site pairs are strictly positive (§3.4). The 2-of-4
   constraint gives a sum rule, not a per-pair sign.
6. **The fresh `W = 0` sampler is useless as a frozen-state probe.** Its
   minimum flippable count over 13,000 cubic draws was 11 (`L = 2`) and 55
   (`L = 3`) — it never goes near the frozen set. Everything informative about
   frozen states at cubic sizes comes from the loop sampler at `n_loops = 1`.
   Any future frozen-density estimate must not be built on the DFS sampler.
7. **The (1,1,1) (111) marginal is exactly flat across its interior**
   (9, 24, 24, 24, 9), so its log-concavity discriminant is 0 — the boundary
   case, satisfied with equality rather than slack.
8. **(1,2,3) carries 453 winding sectors**, not the 217 of (1,2,2) — the
   sector count is not a function of volume, and stage 1's table did not
   record it.
