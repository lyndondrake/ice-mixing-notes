## 1. Corrections and small facts

**The wall picture of the loops report §2 is withdrawn (computed, `h80_diamonds.py`).** Only the
hole-centred half of the projected unit squares carries a cage law; the A-site-centred squares
take every value in [−2, 2] on the odd corpus (516/537 at ±2 at L = 3). A mixed-difference
constraint on a checkerboard of squares has a large kernel (an isolated 2 × 2 block at the right
parity satisfies it), so there is no wall system and §5 item 2 of that report is void. The
correction is written into the report itself.

**Corner counts (computed, `h80`, `h81`, `h82`).** On every odd state #corners_A = #corners_B =
#odd hexagons / 2 (as the cage picture predicts: a corner cage has one A-odd and one B-odd
hexagon). The minimum is 4L per sublattice (12 at L = 3, 16 at L = 4). The minimal states are
two-type states: all A-corners in one z-layer of A-holes with x/y legs (two winding loops of six
corners each), all B-corners in the adjacent layer; type z absent, so H's hypothesis fails
trivially. Two-axis states have corner counts that are multiples of 4L; three-axis states start at
22 and are irregular. Corners do not come in lines along the third axis (`h81`, ~40 %).

**The sign field is a function of the type field (computed, `h94_signcount.py`).** For every
type field tested — odd, curl-free, and the counterexample below — the number of bond states
with that type field (ice + frozen) is exactly 2 (global reversal). This is the Z₂ chain-sense
system: one sense bit per x-chain, one equation per vertex, connected incidence graph; every
sign statement below is therefore secretly type-only, and the solver's `add_sign_field` is
that system.

## 2. Lemma O: the loops are oriented — in odd states only

Orient each rod by its vertex's moment (the in-pair). At a hole O with rods at O ± 2e_k, a rod
points *away* from O iff its sign equals its side.

**Computed (`h83_orient.py`, `h93`, `h95`).** On the 132 sign-carrying corpus states (32 at
L = 3, 100 at L = 4, all odd) every hole of both sublattices has exactly one rod pointing away:
0 exceptions in 58 112 holes. On diversified random frozen samples (random assumptions on 12
bond bits, two seeds, 77 samples): every odd sample (62) obeys it with 0 bad holes; every
curl-free sample (14) violates it, with 48 or 72 bad holes at L = 3.

**Solver.** On the (3,3,3) torus (bond model, ice + non-flippable, `h85`, `h91`): "β = 0" and
"β = 4" at one hole are SAT (β = number of radial cage bonds directed A→B; β = 2 is the oriented
case, and β odd is UNSAT already on the cage by the ice count a = β/2). The model is a genuine
frozen state (432 hexagons with two matches) and is curl-free, with 48 + 12 bad holes. With
"some hexagon odd" added (`h96`), β = 0 and β = 4 are **UNSAT** (192 s, 261 s; control SAT).
So:

**Lemma O (solver, L = 3; computed on 194 odd states at L = 3, 4).** *In an odd frozen state every
hole has one rod pointing toward it and one away; the rod loops of both sublattices are oriented
closed curves.* Consequences (proved from the lemma): each loop's winding vector is canonical;
Σ_loops w = M_S / L for each sublattice; and M_A = M_B in every ice state (proved directly:
along any chain, sources and sinks alternate and lie on alternating sublattices).

**Proved by hand: the corner case.** At a corner cage the two rods lie in one hexagon, which is
odd with both matches on the rods; a hexagon's two flow reversals are one source and one sink,
so one rod points toward O and one away. The whole content of Lemma O is therefore the straight
case: **consecutive kinks v, v + 4e_k on a k-line have equal moment sign** (a straight x-cage is
oriented iff m(v) = m(v′), by the radial count).

**Stronger, with gaps (computed, `h89`).** On all 132 corpus states every k-line's type-k sites
share one moment sign, gaps included (0 mixed lines among 10 000+ with ≥ 2 kinks at L = 4); on
the curl-free samples 24 or 36 lines per state are mixed, each with exactly two sign changes.
Bonded-kink adjacency does not explain it (`h90`: the kinks of a line lie in one component of
the bonded-kink graph in only 84 % of lines).

## 3. The unoriented hole is a type-only pattern (proved, computed)

Bit calculus (proved): bit(e) = 1 iff bond e is directed A→B; along a path, at a vertex u entered
by e and left by e′, bit(e′) = bit(e) ⊕ [τ(u) ≠ axis(e, e′)] (the two hexagon bonds of a match
carry equal bits). For the straight x-cage with rods v = w_x^−, v′ = w_x^+, lower bridgeheads
β, β′ (grid g+1, v's row-neighbours) and upper γ, γ′ (grid g+3, v′'s column-neighbours), a path
v–b–w–b′–v′ gives m(v) = m(v′) iff q = [τ(b) = axis_b] + [τ(w) = axis_w] + [τ(b′) = axis_b′] is
even.

**Computed (`h97_cage_orient.py`, all 3⁸ assignments of the cage under (T2) on its four
hexagons, 144 patterns; the four paths agree on every one).** The straight x-cage is
**unoriented iff its bridgehead pattern is yy/zz or zz/yy**: both lower bridgeheads of one
transverse type and both upper of the other. Every other (T2) pattern (xx/xx, yy/yy, zz/zz,
xy/yx, yx/xy, xz/xz, zx/zx) is oriented. A hand-built yy/zz cage satisfies ice + (T2) on its four
hexagons, and the solver keeps it SAT on balls of radius 6 (`h84`, `h87`): the lemma is not
cage-local.

## 4. Theorem U (solver, L = 3, 4, 5): an unoriented cage forces curl-free

Type-only model (one-hot + (T2)), the cage at O fixed, `h98_unoriented.py`:

| query | L = 3 | L = 4 | L = 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| yy/zz + some hexagon odd | UNSAT 0.2 s | UNSAT 2.8 s | UNSAT 13.9 s |
| zz/yy + some hexagon odd | UNSAT 0.1 s | UNSAT 1.3 s | UNSAT 5.0 s |
| yy/zz alone; yy/yy + odd; straight cage + odd (controls) | SAT | SAT | SAT |

**Theorem U (solver).** *A frozen state containing a straight cage with an unoriented bridgehead
pattern is curl-free.* With §3 and the corner case, Lemma O for odd states follows: odd ⇒ no
unoriented cage ⇒ every hole oriented.

**What the pattern forces (computed, backbone `h99`, enumeration `h102`).** Under yy/zz at one
A-cage on the (3,3,3) and (4,4,4) tori: the whole A x-line through v is type x; the entire
B-grid g+1 is type y and the entire B-grid g+3 is type z; **every B-site of the torus is
non-x**; nothing else on A is forced. The full family at L = 3 has 1008 type fields: in every
one all six B-grids are monochromatic (y or z), B is x-free, A is x plus one of y, z, and there
are no corners — the n_B = 0 curl-free world. So U factors as

  yy/zz ⇒ (V) B-grid g+1 is x-free ⇒ (grid-count identity) B has no type x ⇒
  (sublattice mirror of M′, with the A-kink v; proved) curl-free,

and the new content is (V), equivalently "yy/zz ⇒ B-grid g+1 is a complete type-y plane" (Lemma
S1 then gives B x-free directly).

**(V) is x-global (computed, `h100`, `h101`).** On transverse boxes of radius up to 9 with x-extent
up to ±6, yy/zz forces nothing beyond the cage's own six methylene exclusions. On x-slabs with
the full transverse torus, "some B-site on grid g+1 is type x" (and "not y", and the g+3
mirror) is SAT up to |dx| ≤ 2L−3 and UNSAT from |dx| ≤ 2L−2 at both L = 4 and L = 5: the slab
must contain all hexagons except those meeting a three-grid gap at the antipode. (The "light cone"
seen in `h100` at RX = 5, RT = 10 on L = 5 is a closed-transverse-torus effect — RT = 10 is the
whole plane there; agent uhand shows nothing at all is forced until every transverse plane is
present, and agent ucore that (V) needs the closed three-torus: no window, no tube, no
bounded-x step, and no linear certificate. See `2026-09-22-ucore.md`, `-uhand.md`.)

## 5. What this does for Theorem H

Not a proof. What changes:

- H ⟺ [odd ⇒ some direction missing]. By U (solver) and the hand corner case, **every odd
  state has oriented loops**, so the missing step is: *oriented loops + a corner ⇒ some
  direction missing*, i.e. some axis k on which every k-rod of both sublattices points the same
  way. On the fair samples every odd state does miss a direction, three-axis states miss at most
  one sign per axis; every curl-free sample has all six directions and unoriented holes.
  (An earlier version of this sentence claimed the absent set is the same on A and B; on the
  full corpus it differs in 38/637 and 32/720 odd states — agent extremal — so that claim is
  withdrawn.)
- In an odd state the winding vector of each loop is an invariant, and the line-sign law (§2)
  says each k-line carries one sign. H's contrapositive is then: some axis has all its lines of
  one sign. That is a statement about the type→sign function, which the linear calculus does not
  see; it is the global object the loops report asked for.
- Theorem U is a rigidity theorem of M′'s kind (one local pattern ⇒ a sheet), solver-true in
  seconds where H takes hours, so it is the natural next hand target: a proof of (V) on the
  x-circle would be the first hand proof of an x-global statement in this project.

## 5a. Later the same session: the sign half of H is type-only in odd states (computed)

- **α_k ⟺ one-way (computed, `h104_alpha.py`, 132 odd sign states, 0 exceptions).** Say axis k
  satisfies α_k when on every k-chain consecutive kinks (type-k sites with no type-k site between
  them along the chain) lie on opposite sublattices. One-way ⇒ α_k is proved (an A-kink is +k
  iff it is a source of its chain, a B-kink +k iff a sink, and sources and sinks alternate). On
  every odd state the converse holds too: α_k with type k present ⟺ every k-rod has one sign. On
  a curl-free state it fails (one case). On the type corpus every odd state with all three types
  present has an α axis (396 + 116 of 637 at L = 3; the 125 without are two-type states).
- So, modulo "α ⇒ one-way in odd states", **H ⟺ H_α: all three types present ∧ every axis has a
  chain with two consecutive same-sublattice kinks ⇒ curl-free** — a type-only statement. Solver
  (`h105_halpha.py`, type-only, L = 3): the negation did not finish in 600 s; H_α is H-hard, as
  it must be, so this is a reformulation, not a cheaper target.
- **Adjacent uniform chains agree (computed, `h106_chainpairs.py`).** In odd states, two
  kinked chains of one family joined by a bond (rows in slabs g and g+2 sharing a column bond),
  each uniformly signed, always agree: 0 exceptions in 2 968 + 15 922 pairs. Another
  coherence law of Lemma O's kind, and the mechanism behind α ⇒ one-way.
- **Negative (computed, `h103`, `h107`, `h108`).** One-way is not bond-connectivity of the
  type-k set (one-way axes with up to 21 components; two-way with 2). One-way is not "no loop
  turns back in k": two-way axes with no turn-back loop exist (12 / 40 cases), so the
  disagreement lives across loops. The relative sign of a two-way axis's kink against a one-way
  axis's direction is not a radius-1 local function of the types (85–90 % of kinks in
  sign-determining neighbour patterns, the rest mixed).

**Reading.** In odd states every sign relation examined (adjacent kinks on a line, a whole
line, adjacent chains, α ⇒ one-way) is coherent, and every one fails in curl-free states.
Theorem U is the first of these turned into a type-only rigidity theorem. H's sign half is the
same phenomenon at the scale of a whole axis: for some k the coherence covers every k-kink.
