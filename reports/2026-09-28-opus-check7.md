### Law of line fractions

CONFIRMED.

- Averaging $F_x$ along a $y$-line: the label $y - z$ modulo $4g_x$ of the {0,1}-chain runs uniformly over its $g_x$ classes, since $g_x \mid b$. The {2,3}-chain behaves likewise. - $F_z$ along a $y$-line gives $R(z)$, and $F_y$ along a $z$-line gives $Q(y)$.
- With the constants absorbed per sublattice ($P_S = P + \sigma\alpha$, $Q_S = Q + \sigma(\alpha+\beta)$), the three formulas and their sum 1 follow.

## Corrections and gaps

No gap.

1. **Summary item 2 ('This needs no $K_u$, no Theorem U, no Theorem R1 and no certificate')** is wrong in its last clause. Corollary H uses Theorem $M'$. Theorem $M'$ is proved (note, line 460; check6 §1) with finite lemmas on four hexagons that carry DRAT certificates: PLAQ_K, K4 and, for the first proof, PLAQ_C and EDGE_X. The certificates are window lemmas that pull back to every torus, so they do not limit the scope, but the corollary is not certificate-free. The route through Theorem S2 is free of $M'$ and of certificates, but only on cells with $\gcd(a,b) = \gcd(a,c) = 1$.
2. **Scope observation (not an error).** In Case 2 of Theorem S the two lines meet whenever $\gcd(a,b,c) = 1$: enumerated on (4,3,3), (5,3,3) and (3,4,4), where $\gcd(b,c) \geq 3$. The last step of Lemma J likewise needs only $\gcd(g_y, g_z) = \gcd(a,b,c) = 1$. The hypothesis $\gcd(b,c) = 1$ is used essentially only through (S1), which is what makes $F_x$ a function of the plane. The proof as written is correct. This locates what fails on cells without a coprime pair: (S1), not the meeting.
3. **The counterexample to Lemma T can be smaller.** On $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ every 0/1 function is tri-separable (the span has rank 4). For example, $\varepsilon = \delta_{(0,0)}$ with $\alpha = \beta = (1/2, 0)$ and $\gamma = (0, -1/2)$. Exhaustively, the failing 0/1 patterns number 8, 36 and 96 on $\mathbb{Z}_2^2$, $\mathbb{Z}_3^2$ and $\mathbb{Z}_4^2$. The author's $\mathbb{Z}_3$ example is correct. The record should say that Lemma T fails on every square torus tried, and that its proof is right only for integer jumps.
