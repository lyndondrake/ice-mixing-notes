# Verdict

**FAMILY GROWS** (computed for b = 3, 4, 5; not decided for b >= 6, see T1.4).
Every integral family that closes the LP of the box at one value of b
that I found fails at the next: the 3-vertex family that closes b = 3
leaves 2 feasible branches at b = 4, the 7-vertex family that closes
b = 4 leaves 10 at b = 5, and the 24-vertex set that closes b = 5 is MILP-feasible
at b = 6 and b = 7. The smallest L1 ball of integral vertices about the
next hole O_1 = (6,0,0) that closes the LP has radius 4, 5, 6 for b = 3,
4, 5, but radius 6 still suffices at b = 6, so growth beyond b = 5 is not
established. Holding the family fixed (the 7-vertex family, which closes
both b = 3 and b = 4), the branch certificates split cleanly into two
kinds: **local** certificates (support 4 to 40 rows), identical row for
row at b = 3 and b = 4, and **global** certificates whose support takes
every y-value of the closed circle and whose support, L1 norm and number
of distinct multiplier values grow with b (the all-x branch: 118 rows, L1
199, 22 values at b = 3; 283 rows, L1 1367/2, 58 values at b = 4). No
rule uniform in b is visible in the global ones. A closed form, if there
is one, has to be sought in sums over the closed y-lines (the parameter
that grows), not in branch certificates on a fixed integral family.

# Statement tested

Exactly h633's box lemma, type model (h360_lib), cell (a, b, c), O =
(2,0,0). Variables X, Y, Z >= 0 at every vertex lying on a box hexagon
(vertices on no box hexagon carry only their own one-hot row, which is
always satisfiable, so they are dropped). Equality rows:

- one-hot X + Y + Z = 1 at each such vertex;
- (T2) linear: the sum of the six matching indicators equals 2, on every
  hexagon all six of whose vertices have -3 <= x - 2 <= 2b+1 and
  |z| <= 2b-1 (y unrestricted, the y-circle of length 4b closed);
- the four literals X(v_0) = X(v_1) = 1, Y(beta) = 1, Y(gamma') = 0 with
  v_0 = (0,0,0), v_1 = (4,0,0), beta = (1,1,1), gamma' = (3,-1,1);
- the negated conclusion X(v_2) = 0, v_2 = (8,0,0);
- on a branch, rows t(u,k) = 1 ("u has type k") for the family vertices.

Cells (12,3,8), (12,4,8), (12,5,6), (12,6,8), (12,7,8): the box does not
wrap (4a > 2b+5, 4c > 4b-1). Positions below are **relative to O**
(absolute = relative + (2,0,0)) unless marked absolute.

**Symmetry group** (proved by inspection). The lattice symmetries that
fix O, the x-axis and its direction, and the box are the identity, the
C2 rotation (x,y,z) -> (x,-y,-z) about the rod axis, and the two mirrors
exchanging y and z (which exchange types y and z). The mirrors move
Y(beta) = 1 to a Z literal; C2 maps the hypothesis pair {beta, gamma'} to
the other pair {beta' = (1,-1,-1), gamma = (3,1,-1)} (cubethree's "by
symmetry"). So the symmetry group of the hypothesis set is **trivial**,
and no symmetry was imposed on the duals. Explored: with X(v_2) = 0 the
LP itself forces Y(beta') = 1 and Y(gamma) = 0 (h684 maxima), so the LP
closure of the hypotheses is C2-symmetric; I did not exploit this.

A branch whose control is infeasible is excluded by the hypotheses
alone (its certificate does not use X(v_2) = 0); those with a feasible
control use the negated conclusion. The JSON files hold, per branch, every
support row with its multiplier and label (kind; vertex or hexagon hole

Rows by kind: every certificate uses only one-hot, (T2), literal,
negation and branch rows (there are no bound rows: the variables are
non-negative and the positive reduced columns play the role of the r_j of

**Uniqueness of the canonical form: not checked.** Least L1 need not be
unique; I added no secondary tie-break. The local certificates agree
exactly between two independent LPs (b = 3 and b = 4), which is weak
evidence of uniqueness for them; for the global ones it is open.

What I read from this (explored):

1. **The local certificates are b-independent lemmas.** Nine branches have
   the same certificate, row for row and multiplier for multiplier, at b = 3
   and b = 4, inside x in [-1, 6] and |z| <= 3, multipliers in {+-1/2,
   +-1, +-2, ...} with at most 9 distinct values. Example (F7, both b,
   branch (4,0,+-2) = x, (4,2,0) = z; 40 rows): (T2) at the hexagons of
   hole (0,0,0) with multipliers 3, 1, 2 for sigma = (-1,-1,1), (-1,1,-1),
   (1,-1,-1), of hole (4,0,0) with -1, -1 for (1,-1,-1), (1,1,1), of holes
   (2,+-2,0) with +-1/2 on all four sigma; literals -4 X(v_0), -2 X(v_1),
   -4 Y(beta), +2 Y(gamma'), +2 for X(v_2) = 0; one-hot -5/2 at (1,-1,1),
   -2 at (-1,-1,-1), -1 at (0,+-2,0), -1/2 at (1,-3,-1), (1,1,-1),
   (1,3,1), +1/2 at (3,-1,-1), (3,-3,1), (3,1,1), (3,3,-1), +1 at
   (4,+-2,0), (4,0,+-2), (5,-1,1), (5,1,-1). These are finite statements about the
   cages of O and O_1 and need no closed form.
2. **The global certificates are where R lives**, and they are the ones
   that use the whole circle. Their support grows by about a factor 2.4
   from b = 3 to b = 4 (118 to 283 rows for the all-x branch, about a third
   of the box's rows in each case), their L1 norm by 3.4 to 5.8, their
   distinct multiplier values by 2 to 3.3, and their denominators from 2
   to 24. No multiplier pattern repeats between b = 3 and b = 4, and I did
   not find a rule tying a multiplier to its position relative to O, the
   rod line or the box edges.
3. So the analogue of Theorem 9's four rules was not found, and the
   identity step of T3 was not reached: there was no rule to verify by
   formula.

# What is left and a stop rule

1. **Do not** repeat: the A x-lines through O +- 2e_y in the box (fail);
   least-L1 branch certificates on a fixed vertex family (the global ones
   do not stabilise in b); MILP deletion over the whole box at b >= 5
   (individual MILPs exceed 5 s and the filter does not finish in 10
   minutes).
2. **The one open datum that could reverse the verdict:** whether the
   integral ball of radius 6 about O_1 closes the LP at b = 7 and b = 8
   (the MILP did not finish in 90 s at b = 7). If it does, a bounded family
   exists (at most 60 vertices, which is still too many branches to
   tabulate by hand, so the practical conclusion would not change much).
   One run of at most 10 minutes per b, then stop.
3. **Where a closed form should be sought instead.** Every global
   certificate spans the full y-circle; this agrees with rodline's reading
   (sums of P^y over closed y-lines of holes, whose methylene terms
   telescope, as in the proof of Theorem 5). The thing to tabulate is the
   certificate projected onto closed y-lines: for each (x, z) the sum of
   multipliers of the rows on the y-line of holes or vertices at (x, z).
   If those line sums are uniform in b while the in-line distribution is
   not (least L1 spreads mass arbitrarily along a line whose rows are
   translates of one another), the closed form exists in the line sums.
   That is a computation on the existing JSON files (no new solving), and
   it is the next step if anyone continues; stop if the projected sums of
   the all-x branch differ between b = 3 and b = 4 beyond translation.
4. The C2 symmetry of the LP closure (T2 note) could be imposed after
   adding Y(beta') = 1 and Y(gamma) = 0 as derived rows; it would halve the
   search but not change the growth.
