## Corrections

**C1. The orientation of the bond row is part of the closed form.** The multiplier +¼·min(4, n_E) is correct only for the row written with the A-end first: d·n(a) − d·n(a + d), where a ∈ A and d ∈ {d₀, …, d₃}. All other rows are written as (left side − right side). With the opposite orientation the combination fails at every s: 6, 30 and 66 wrong-sign columns at s = 5, 7, 9. The report's parenthesis in Lemma 3 ('β = −¼, in the y-sign of the scripts') points at this clash but does not resolve it. **Repair:** in §0 row 1 and §1, state 'bond row d·n(a) − d·n(a+d) = 0 with a the A end'. In that convention the identity reads Σ λ_i(A_i x − b_i) = −1 − r·x with r ≥ 0, which is the convention of §1, and the four r-entries are the corner in-rods. That is all that changes.

**C2. Lemma B (iii), bound.** For A-vertices the proof ends '2(n_a − n_b) ≥ 8 − |a_w| − |b_w| ≥ 4'. Under the hypothesis |a_w| + |b_w| = 2·max(|dx|, |dy|) ≤ 2, the right side is ≥ 6. The stated '≥ 4' is true, but it is not the bound the hypothesis gives. It is also what makes the A-vertex case (iii) impossible at (2,0), where |a_w| + |b_w| = 4. The conclusion is unaffected, because n_a − n_b ≤ 1. The B-vertex version, 2(n_a − n_b) ≤ |a_w| + |b_w| − 4, re-derived: at L_in, b = −2η(J+1); at U_ε, a = a_w − 2ε(n_a − J); then 4J ≤ 2n_b − 4 + |b_w| and 4J ≥ 2n_a − |a_w|. It is correct. It is attained at (2,0) exactly when n is even, and that holds for every admissible s at (2,0), since s ≡ 2 (mod 4). The sharpness remark should read 'from the first separation', not 's = 10'.

**C3. §3.1 is written for h_v ∈ HA only.** From a B-hole the first step changes b, so j_a(t) = ⌊t/2⌋ and j_b(t) = ⌈t/2⌉, and likewise for n_a and n_b. The HA formula applied to B-starts disagrees with the true D in 225 of 476 test pairs. The report reaches v ∈ B only through the rotary reflection (x,y,z) ↦ (y,−x,−z) plus the global flip of the state. That route is valid: the map fixes A, maps DIRS to DIRS, acts on n as a vector (so every row is preserved), and the flip preserves orientation in real states (law C). So this is a scope note, not a gap. Lemma B and the certificate were also checked directly for v ∈ B.

## Periodisation (task 4)

Let a state on the (L,L,L) torus have all holes of the projected D oriented, and let v, w be as in the statement with some lift w̃ − v = (dx, dy, s), s > 0. Lift the state to a periodic state x̃ of the lattice. Every lattice row whose projection is a torus row holds at x̃: one-hot, (T2), bond, and Lemma O at the lifts of the projected holes of D. The hypotheses hold at v and w̃. The identity is a finite formal identity, so evaluating it at x̃ gives 0 = −1 − r·x̃ ≤ −1.

Distinct lattice rows with the same projection cause no problem. Every row is an equality, so multipliers of any sign may be summed. The projected combination has torus columns that are sums of lattice columns of one sign, and the same constant. Projected rows are never degenerate for L ≥ 2 (4L ≥ 8): the six hexagon vertices are distinct, and so are the six methylenes of a hole. v ≠ w on the torus except in the same-line case with s ≡ 0 (mod 4L), where the hypotheses already contradict one-hot. So D may wrap round the torus any number of times. The only input needed is orientation of the projected holes of D, and in an odd state that is every hole. Every z-difference mod 4L has a positive lift, and every transverse offset (±1,±1) mod 4L lifts to exactly (±1,±1). So the torus statement covers every pair.
